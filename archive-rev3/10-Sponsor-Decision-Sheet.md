# DOCUMENT 10 — SPONSOR DECISION SHEET

These are the genuine policy choices that remain after research. In each case, Option A is what the
draft does.

---

**1. The 50/50 presumption on a restored parent's schedule motion**
- **A.** It applies to the *first* schedule motion filed with or after restoration of status.
- **B.** No presumption. Both parents stand equal, but there is no starting point.
- **Recommendation: A.** Without it, the status quo controls in practice and "equal status" is a
  label. The one-motion limit is the floodgate answer.

**2. The enforcement body**
- **A.** County CSEAs, with determinations entered by the court absent an objection.
- **B.** A court-housed office on the Michigan Friend-of-the-Court model.
- **Recommendation: A.** It moves the work out of the courts, and Ohio already uses this exact
  mechanism for support review (R.C. 3119.63–.65). B is constitutionally easier but leaves the
  workload with judges.

**3. Agency first, or the parent's choice**
- **A.** A parent may use the agency *or* file contempt directly.
- **B.** The agency route is required before a contempt filing, except in emergencies.
- **Recommendation: A at enactment.** Revisit B after two years of Section 5 data. Requiring
  exhaustion before the agencies are staffed would delay relief.

**4. Before any order: a no-removal rule**
- **A.** No rule. Unmarried parents are treated exactly like married parents before divorce.
- **B.** Add to R.C. 3109.03, for *all* parents: "Until a court issues an order, neither parent shall
  remove the child from the other parent's care without consent, except to prevent imminent harm."
- **Recommendation: A, with B ready if domestic-violence advocates press the grab-and-go objection.**
  B answers that objection directly, but it creates a new rule for married parents too.

**5. Proof standard for a knowingly false allegation**
- **A.** Preponderance, with three elements: material falsity, knowledge, and purpose to interfere.
- **B.** Clear and convincing.
- **Recommendation: A.** The three elements are the real protection. B is a reasonable trade if it
  moves domestic-violence organizations to neutrality.

**6. Agency funding**
- **A.** State appropriation plus federal Access and Visitation grants (as drafted).
- **B.** Add a cost fee charged to a parent found to have denied time.
- **Recommendation: A.** A fee invites the argument that it is a penalty, and penalties for
  disobeying a court order belong to the courts. Costs are already recoverable through contempt.

**7. The 90-overnight floor**
- **A.** Keep it.
- **B.** Remove it and rely on "maximize" alone, as Kentucky, West Virginia, Missouri, and Florida do.
- **Recommendation: A.** Without a number, "maximize" is unreviewable, and every other weekend
  survives rebuttal unexamined. 90 equals Minnesota's enacted 25 percent.

---

*These were settled by research and are not open: drafting against current law; leaving R.C.
2919.23(A)(1) intact; a 45-day continuance; a 36-month outside limit; no agency penalties or contempt
power; prosecutorial discretion preserved.*
