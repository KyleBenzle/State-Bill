# DOCUMENT 9 — COURT-WORKLOAD MEMO

**The design test:** does a provision remove a decision that someone now has to ask a judge to make?

## 1. Work the bill removes from judges and magistrates

| Today | Under the bill | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Every missed weekend means a contempt motion: a summons, a hearing, testimony, and a ruling | A parent files with the county agency. Unopposed determinations are **entered without a hearing** (the support-review model, R.C. 3119.65) | 3109.047 |
| Make-up time is argued case by case ("may … if in best interest") | It is **mandatory**, same type and duration, and computed by the agency | 3109.051(K)(1); 3109.047(D) |
| The court reconstructs years of texts and police reports | The agency files **one standardized record** per order | 3109.047(G) |
| Contested custody begins from an open field | It begins from a **50/50 presumption**. Predictable starting points produce settlements; that was the experience the sponsor of H.B. 14 described ("we don't fight over time any more") | 3109.044 |
| An agreed plan needs an affirmative best-interest finding | The court **approves unless** it finds the plan not in the child's best interest | 3109.044(C)(1) |
| An unmarried father must sue to obtain custodial status | **Status by operation of law** | 3109.042(A) |
| A GAL's or parenting coordinator's informal "suspensions" lead to emergency motions | They are **void by statute**. Only court orders count | 3109.045 |
| The same allegation is relitigated, or left pending, for years | **One period, one resolution.** No repackaging | 3109.046(D) |
| Disputes over whether "time on a temporary order" justifies permanence | Settled by statute: **no inference** | 3109.043(C) |
| Plan drafting from scratch | **Statewide forms** | Section 5(A) |

## 2. What moves to administrative enforcement

The county CSEA handles:
- intake;
- obtaining the order;
- notice and response;
- determining a denial of a **clear** order;
- calculating make-up time;
- keeping the record;
- referral to the prosecutor;
- filing contempt for the parent at the threshold.

## 3. What remains judicial

- Contempt and jail.
- Custody and status changes.
- Safety and abuse findings.
- Protection orders.
- Unclear orders.
- Objections to agency determinations (heard anew within 28 days).
- Criminal guilt.
- First allocations.
- Equal-status motions.

## 4. New work the bill creates

| New work | Estimate | Offset |
|---|---|---|
| Equal-status motions under 3109.04(E)(1)(c) | A one-time wave of existing-order cases; most are paper motions granted on notice, since the grounds to deny are narrow | Many will be joined with a schedule motion that *Braatz* already allows. One presumption-backed schedule motion per family |
| Presumption-backed schedule motions | Real hearings | Limited to the first motion; later motions follow ordinary law |
| Objections to agency determinations | A fraction of determinations; Minnesota and Michigan experience suggests most go unopposed (not quantified here) | Each objection replaces a contempt hearing that would have occurred anyway |
| Entering unopposed determinations | Ministerial review ("unless contrary to law") | — |
| Restricted-parent hearings within 90 days, and the 30-month court-set hearings | Pulls existing cases forward rather than adding cases | Ends indefinite restrictions that produce repeated motions |
| GAL cross-examination | Marginal; GALs already testify when called | — |

## 5. Is the net effect realistically workload-reducing?

**For courts: yes, probably modestly, after a transition bump.**
- **The bump.** Status motions, plus first schedule motions, in families with old sole-custody
  orders. This is concentrated in the first two to three years.
- **The steady-state savings** come from three sources:
  1. routine parenting-time contempt moving to the agency;
  2. predictable first allocations that settle more often;
  3. allegation and GAL disputes that end by rule instead of by motion.
- **The cost moves to counties.** CSEAs bear a real new cost. It is not federally reimbursable under
  Title IV-D. The bill names state appropriations and Access and Visitation grants (42 U.S.C. 669b),
  and delays the agency's start by one year (Section 3(E)).
- **Expect a fiscal note.** LSC will produce one, and the county cost is the item opponents will
  emphasize.

**What would make the savings larger, and was rejected:**
- requiring the agency route before any contempt filing (an exhaustion rule);
- letting the agency find contempt.

The first delays urgent cases. The second is unconstitutional (*Hale*; *Zakany*). See Document 10,
Decision 3.
