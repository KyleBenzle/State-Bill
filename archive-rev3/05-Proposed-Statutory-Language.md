## TITLE

To amend sections 2705.031, 2919.23, 3109.03, 3109.04, 3109.042, 3109.043, 3109.051, 3109.12, and
3109.401 and to enact sections 3109.044, 3109.045, 3109.046, and 3109.047 of the Revised Code to
give every legal parent equal parental status; to establish a presumption of shared parenting with
substantially equal parenting time; to let a parent under an existing order regain equal status; to
provide that only a court may change a parenting order; to require that allegations restricting a
parent be resolved; and to provide administrative enforcement of parenting time orders.

# PART TWO — THE STARTING POINT

## Sec. 3109.044 — enacted. Shared parenting and substantially equal time.

**(A)** When a court first allocates parental rights and responsibilities for a child, including by a
temporary order, there is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the evidence, that shared
parenting with substantially equal parenting time is in the child's best interest.
"Substantially equal" means each parent has at least forty-five per cent of overnights, or the
equivalent, over a year.

**(B)** If the presumption is not rebutted, the court shall issue a shared parenting decree under
which both parents are residential parents and legal custodians, major decisions are made jointly,
and parenting time is substantially equal. No party needs to file a plan. If none is filed, the
court shall issue one.

**(C)** The presumption does not apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the parents agree. The court shall approve their agreement unless it finds the
agreement is not in the child's best interest;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** As to a parent against whom a protection order or consent agreement under section
2151.34, 2903.213, 2903.214, 2919.26, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code protects the other parent or
the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** As to a parent described in the second paragraph of division (C) of section 3109.04 of
the Revised Code.

**(D)** None of the following, by itself, rebuts the presumption: conflict between the parents; one
parent's objection; a lack of cooperation caused by the parent who relies on it; or an allegation the
court has not found true.

**(E)** If the presumption is rebutted, the court shall:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** State in writing the facts that rebut it;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Unless the parents agree, order a schedule that maximizes each parent's time with the
child, consistent with the child's welfare;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Not give a parent fewer than ninety overnights a year, or the equivalent, unless the
parents agree, that parent asks for less, distance makes it impracticable, or the court finds by a
preponderance of the evidence that more time would endanger the child's physical or emotional
welfare. In that case, the court shall order the least restrictive arrangement that protects the
child and state its reasons in writing. **Ninety overnights is a floor, not a standard or target.**

**(F)** The court may find the presumption rebutted as to parenting time but not as to joint
decision-making, or the reverse. Each parent makes day-to-day and emergency decisions while the child
is with that parent. If the parents cannot agree on a major decision, either may ask the court to
decide it, and the existing arrangement continues until the court does.

**(G)** A court may restrict a parent's rights or parenting time to protect the child or a parent
from physical harm, abuse, or domestic violence on a finding by a preponderance of the evidence. A
criminal charge or conviction is not required.

# PART THREE — ONLY A COURT CHANGES A COURT ORDER

## Sec. 3109.045 — enacted. Court orders control.

**(A)** An order allocating parental rights and responsibilities or providing for parenting time stays
in effect as written until a court changes it. No one else may change it, suspend it, or add
conditions to it. That includes a parent, guardian ad litem, parenting coordinator, mediator,
evaluator, counselor, therapist, law enforcement officer, and any employee of a public children
services agency or child support enforcement agency. Their recommendations, reports, allegations,
safety plans, and instructions do not change the order. Parents may agree to change a particular
exchange or period. That agreement does not change the order.

**(B)** A guardian ad litem may investigate, interview, observe, obtain information, report, testify,
and make recommendations as provided by law and the Rules of Superintendence. A recommendation is
evidence. It has no effect unless the court adopts it in an order. A party may call the guardian ad
litem as a witness and cross-examine the guardian ad litem about the report and the basis for any
recommendation. A court shall not give a guardian ad litem, or anyone else who is not a judge or
magistrate, authority to change, suspend, supervise, or condition parenting time or the allocation
of parental rights and responsibilities. A provision of an order that purports to give that
authority is unenforceable.

**(C)** A parenting coordinator may help parents carry out an order and decide disputes about how it
is carried out, within the authority given by the appointing order and the Rules of Superintendence.
A parenting coordinator may not reduce or suspend parenting time, require supervision, or change the
allocation of parental rights and responsibilities. On a parent's objection, the court shall review
the decision independently.

**(D)** A public children services agency safety plan restricts a parent's court-ordered parenting
time only if that parent agrees in writing or a court orders it. An agency that believes a child is
in danger may seek an emergency order under Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code.

**(E)** Nothing in sections 3109.044 to 3109.047 of the Revised Code limits a protection order, taking
a child into custody under section 2151.31 of the Revised Code, an emergency or ex parte court order,
or action reasonably necessary to prevent imminent physical harm to a person.

# PART FOUR — ALLEGATIONS MUST BE RESOLVED

## Sec. 3109.046 — enacted. Allegations; outside limit; false allegations.

**(A)** In this section:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** "Restriction" means a court order, or part of one, that suspends, requires supervision
of, or materially reduces a parent's parenting time or parental rights and responsibilities because
of an allegation of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or other misconduct by that parent.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** An allegation is "resolved" when a court, after a hearing at which the restricted
parent could present evidence, finds whether it is true. The finding may come from the court that
issued the restriction, an adjudication under Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code, a full hearing on a
protection order, or a criminal conviction. None of the following, by itself, resolves an
allegation: an agency's determination, a decision whether to charge, a dismissal, or an acquittal.

**(B)** An allegation, report, or filing does not by itself change a parenting order. Only a court
order based on evidence can impose a restriction.

**(C)** On the restricted parent's request, the court shall hold an evidentiary hearing on the
allegation within ninety days unless good cause is shown. A pending investigation or criminal case,
or the absence of a charge, does not by itself prevent the court from deciding whether the
allegation is true.

**(D)** A restriction based on an unresolved allegation ends thirty-six months after it first took
effect. The following rules apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the allegation is unresolved at thirty months, the court shall set it for hearing
on its own motion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Before the thirty-six months end, the court may grant one continuance of not more than
forty-five days, on written findings that additional time is genuinely necessary.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Delay requested or caused by the restricted parent is not counted.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** Every allegation about conduct that occurred before the restriction took effect shares
the restriction's thirty-six-month period, however the allegation is later described. Only conduct
after that date can support a new restriction with a new period.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** When the restriction ends, the order that applied before it resumes. If there was
none, the court shall issue a parenting time order under section 3109.044 of the Revised Code within
twenty-eight days, without relying on the unresolved allegation.

**(E)** If the court finds the allegation true, it may continue, change, or end the restriction in the
child's best interest. If it finds the allegation not true, the restriction ends.

**(F)** An allegation or report made in good faith shall not be held against the person who made it.
An allegation is not false merely because it was unsubstantiated, not prosecuted, dismissed, not
proven, or mistaken. A parent's good-faith action to protect the child or the parent from abuse or
domestic violence shall not be counted against that parent under division (F)(1)(f) of section
3109.04 or division (D)(10) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(G)** If the court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that a parent made an allegation that was
materially false, that the parent knew was false, and that the parent made in order to interfere
with the other parent's relationship or parenting time with the child, the court shall order
compensatory parenting time for the time lost and the parent's payment of the other parent's
reasonable attorney's fees and costs. The finding is a change in circumstances for purposes of
division (E)(1)(a) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code.

**(H)** This section does not govern a protection order proceeding or a case under Chapter 2151. of
the Revised Code. It does govern a restriction in a case under this chapter that relies on either of
them. It does not limit section 2151.421 of the Revised Code.

# PART FIVE — ENFORCEMENT

## Sec. 3109.047 — enacted. Parenting time enforcement agency.

**(A) Agency.** The child support enforcement agency for each county is its parenting time enforcement
agency. The agency administers parenting time orders. It does not decide custody, safety, or
contempt.

**(B) Complaint.** A parent may file a complaint with the agency of the county where the child lives,
on a form prescribed by the department of job and family services, within sixty days after court-
ordered parenting time was missed. The complaint identifies the order and the missed time. There is
no filing fee.

**(C) Inquiry.** Within fourteen days after receiving the complaint, the agency shall obtain the order
from the court and send the complaint to the other parent. The other parent shall have fourteen days
to respond in writing. The agency may contact both parents, gather information, and offer a
conference by any means.

**(D) Determination.** Within forty-five days after receiving the complaint, the agency shall issue a
written determination. If the order clearly provides the parenting time, the time was not provided,
and the other parent has not shown a lawful excuse under division (K)(3) of section 3109.051 of the
Revised Code, the agency shall find that parenting time was denied and shall specify compensatory
parenting time of the same type and duration. The compensatory time is to be taken within one year,
at times the wronged parent chooses on at least seven days' notice. Otherwise, the agency shall find
no denial. A parent who does not respond is treated as having shown no lawful excuse.

**(E) Matters for the court.** The agency shall make no determination, and shall file the complaint
and its record with the court, if either of the following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The order is unclear as to the time in question;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A parent asserts in good faith that the parenting time would endanger the child.

The court shall treat the filing as a contempt action under division (K) of section 3109.051 of the
Revised Code.

**(F) Effect.** The agency shall send the determination to both parents by ordinary mail, and by
any electronic means a parent has provided, and shall file it with the court.
Either parent may object by filing with the court within fourteen days. The court shall hear an
objection within twenty-eight days and decide the matter anew. If no one objects, the court shall
enter the determination as its order unless it is contrary to law. An entered determination is
enforceable like any other court order.

**(G) Record.** The agency shall keep one record for each parenting time order. The record shows every
complaint, response, determination, objection, and outcome, and whether compensatory time was
provided. The agency shall file the record in any court proceeding about parenting time or the
allocation of parental rights and responsibilities under that order.

**(H) Repeated or serious conduct.** The agency shall refer a matter to the prosecutor under division
(F) of section 2919.23 of the Revised Code, and may begin a contempt action for the wronged parent
under section 2705.031 of the Revised Code, if any of the following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Within twelve months, three denials by the same parent under the same order have
been entered by the court under division (F) of this section or found in contempt;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A denial lasted fourteen or more consecutive days;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The child was concealed or removed from this state.

**(I) Limits.** The agency may not change an order, impose any penalty, or decide whether a parent is
in contempt. Its activities under this section are not Title IV-D activities and shall be funded and
accounted for separately.

# PART SEVEN — UNCODIFIED SECTIONS

**Section 3. Existing orders.**
- (A) Sections 3109.043 and 3109.044 of the Revised Code, as enacted or amended by this act, apply to
  any allocation decided on or after the effective date, including in a pending case.
- (B) Orders in effect on the effective date stay in effect. A parent may seek equal status under
  division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code. Otherwise, enactment of this act is not
  grounds to modify an order.
- (C) For a restriction in effect on the effective date, the thirty-six-month period under section
  3109.046 of the Revised Code ends on the later of thirty-six months after the restriction took
  effect or twelve months after the effective date.
- (D) Sections 3109.047 and 2919.23 and division (K) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code, as
  enacted or amended by this act, apply to conduct on or after the effective date, whatever the date
  of the order.
- (E) Section 3109.047 of the Revised Code takes effect one year after the effective date of this
  act.

**Section 4. Child support.** This act does not change how child support is calculated. An order under
division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code is not by itself grounds to modify child
support.

**Section 5. Forms, funding, and data.**
- (A) The General Assembly requests that the Supreme Court adopt statewide forms for a shared parenting
  plan and a motion under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code.
- (B) The department of job and family services shall adopt the complaint and determination forms
  under section 3109.047 of the Revised Code. It shall fund the section from state appropriations and
  any federal access and visitation grant under 42 U.S.C. 669b.
- (C) The General Assembly requests that the Supreme Court publish each year:
  - the number of initial allocations, and how many ordered substantially equal time;
  - the number of motions under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code, and how
    many were granted;
  - the number of agency complaints, determinations, objections, and referrals;
  - the number of prosecutorial declinations.

**Section 6. S.B. 174.** If Sub. S.B. 174 of the 136th General Assembly is enacted, the General Assembly
intends this act to be conformed to it.
