### The bill in nine lines

1. **Parents start equal.** Once paternity is established, both parents are equal custodians, married
   or not. Status is not a schedule.
2. **50/50 start.** A rebuttable presumption at every first allocation; agreed plans approved unless
   harmful.
3. **If rebutted,** written reasons, maximize each parent's time, and a 90-overnight floor that is not
   a target.
4. **Old orders.** Equal status by motion, with no change in circumstances needed. The schedule is
   unchanged. The first schedule motion starts at 50/50.
5. **Only a court changes a court order.** A GAL recommends and can be cross-examined. Parenting
   coordinators handle implementation only.
6. **Allegations must be resolved:**
   - a hearing within 90 days on request;
   - a 36-month outside limit, with a court-set hearing at 30 months;
   - one 45-day continuance;
   - no clock-restarting by rewording.
7. **County CSEAs enforce routine violations.** The agency issues a determination and sets make-up
   time; the court enters it unless a parent objects. Unclear orders and safety claims go to judges.
8. **Criminal.** Existing R.C. 2919.23 is kept intact. The bill states that withholding court-ordered
   time is "without privilege" and is not "a civil matter."
9. **Referral.** The agency refers serious cases. The prosecutor charges or sends a written
   declination within 60 (+30) days. No reasons are required.

### Research limits in this revision

The Revision 3 research agents stopped early on a billing error. The primary texts they had saved
were read directly. These items were **not** completed:
- floodgate data from other states;
- several state administrative-enforcement programs (Oregon, Indiana, Arizona, Utah, Texas);
- the controlling federal Title IV-D regulation;
- Ohio's Access and Visitation grant details;
- which counties, if any, house their CSEA with the prosecutor.
