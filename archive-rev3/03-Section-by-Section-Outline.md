# DOCUMENT 3 — SECTION-BY-SECTION OUTLINE (REVISION 3)

The bill amends 9 existing sections, enacts 4 new ones, and has 4 uncodified sections. New sections
are in **bold**.

| Part | What it does | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| One | Parents start equal | 3109.03, 3109.042 |
| Two | The 50/50 starting point; equal status under old orders | **3109.044**; 3109.04, 3109.043, 3109.12, 3109.401 |
| Three | Only a court changes a court order | **3109.045** |
| Four | Allegations must be resolved | **3109.046** |
| Five | Enforcement | **3109.047**; 3109.051(K); 2705.031 |
| Six | Criminal interference and referral | 2919.23 |
| Seven | Transition, child support, forms, funding, data, S.B. 174 | Uncodified 3–6 |

## PART ONE

**3109.03:** the equality rule now covers "the parents of a child," married or not. There is no
preference by sex or by marital status.

**3109.042:**
- (A) The mother is sole custodian until paternity is established. After that, **both parents are
  residential parents and legal custodians**, with no lawsuit needed.
- (B) This is status, not a schedule.
- (C) Existing orders keep governing; the parent uses the 3109.04(E)(1)(c) motion.
- (D) The rape and sexual-battery bar, now sex-neutral.

## PART TWO

**3109.044 (new):**
- (A) The presumption: shared parenting with substantially equal time (45 percent or more of
  overnights) at every first allocation, including temporary orders. Rebuttable by a preponderance
  of the evidence.
- (B) If not rebutted: a shared parenting decree. No plan needs to be filed; the court issues one.
- (C) The presumption does not apply:
  - when the parents agree (the agreement is **approved unless** found not in the child's best
    interest);
  - to a protection-order respondent;
  - to a 3109.04(C) offender.
- (D) Not enough on its own to rebut: conflict, objection, self-created non-cooperation, or an
  unfound allegation.
- (E) If rebutted:
  - written facts;
  - a schedule that maximizes each parent's time;
  - a **90-overnight floor** with four exceptions (agreement, the parent's own request, distance,
    endangerment);
  - least restrictive means below the floor;
  - "a floor, not a standard or target."
- (F) Time and decision-making are rebutted separately. Day-to-day and emergency decisions belong to
  the parent who has the child. On deadlock, the status quo continues until the court decides.
- (G) Safety restrictions on a preponderance. No conviction is required.

**3109.04:**
- (A) Allocation follows 3109.044.
- **(E)(1)(c): equal status under an existing order.**
  - No change in circumstances is needed, and no plan.
  - The court **shall grant** unless it finds a proven history of violence or abuse, an immediate
    threat, or a statutory bar.
  - The result is a shared parenting decree. **The schedule, support, and school stay unchanged.**
    There is no self-help.
  - **The first schedule motion** filed with or after restoration gets the 50/50 presumption.
  - After a denial, there is a 12-month wait to refile.

**3109.043:** 28-day oral hearing on request. No inference from a temporary order or its length.

**3109.12:** the presumption applies to parenting-time orders under this section.

**3109.401(A)(4)(e):** the "no standard schedule" policy is made subject to 3109.044.

## PART THREE

**3109.045 (new):**
- (A) An order stands as written until a court changes it. No GAL, parenting coordinator, counselor,
  caseworker, officer, or parent can change it. Parents may agree to swap a particular exchange or
  period.
- (B) A GAL investigates, interviews, observes, reports, testifies, and recommends. A recommendation
  is evidence only. **A party may call and cross-examine the GAL.** Delegating power over parenting
  time to a non-judge is unenforceable.
- (C) A parenting coordinator handles implementation disputes only. Independent court review on
  objection.
- (D) A safety plan binds court-ordered time only with the parent's written consent or a court order.
- (E) One savings clause for the whole act: protection orders, emergency custody, ex parte orders,
  and action to prevent imminent harm.

## PART FOUR

**3109.046 (new):**
- (A) Definitions of "restriction" and "resolved." A court finding after a hearing resolves an
  allegation. An agency determination, a charging decision, a dismissal, or an acquittal does not.
- (B) An allegation does not change an order by itself.
- (C) A hearing within 90 days on the restricted parent's request. A pending investigation or
  criminal case is no bar to deciding.
- (D) The outside limit:
  - 36 months;
  - the court sets a hearing itself at 30 months;
  - **one 45-day continuance**;
  - **the restricted parent's own delay is not counted**;
  - **all allegations about conduct before the restriction share one period**;
  - the prior order then resumes.
- (E) If the allegation is found true, the restriction may continue. If not, it ends.
- (F) Good-faith reports are protected. Unsubstantiated, dismissed, or mistaken does not mean false.
  Protective action is not "failure to facilitate."
- (G) A knowingly false allegation (falsity, knowledge, and purpose) brings make-up time, fees, and a
  change in circumstances.
- (H) Scope: protection-order and Chapter 2151 proceedings themselves are excluded. 2151.421 is
  preserved.

## PART FIVE

**3109.047 (new), the parenting time enforcement agency (the county CSEA):**
- (A) The agency administers orders. It does not decide custody, safety, or contempt.
- (B) Complaint on a state form within 60 days of the missed time. No fee.
- (C) Within 14 days the agency obtains the order and notifies the other parent, who has 14 days to
  respond. The agency may offer a conference.
- (D) A determination within 45 days. For a **clear** order with no lawful excuse shown, the agency
  finds a denial and sets make-up time: same type and duration, within one year, at times the wronged
  parent chooses on 7 days' notice. Not responding means no excuse was shown.
- (E) Unclear orders and good-faith safety claims go to the court, which treats them as contempt
  actions.
- (F) The determination is mailed and filed. A parent may object within 14 days; the court hears the
  objection within 28 days and decides anew. If there is no objection, **the court enters it** unless
  it is contrary to law.
- (G) **One enforcement record per order**, filed in later proceedings.
- (H) Referral to the prosecutor, and optional contempt filing, when any of these occurs:
  - three entered denials in 12 months;
  - a denial of 14 or more consecutive days;
  - concealment or removal from Ohio.
- (I) No changing orders, no penalties, no contempt. Separately funded; not Title IV-D.

**3109.051(K):**
- (1) Make-up time is mandatory on a contempt finding: same type and duration, within one year.
- (2) A 28-day hearing. The prima facie case is the order, the time owed, and evidence it didn't
  happen.
- (3) The single list of lawful excuses.
- (4) Child refusal: reasonable efforts, no force.
- (5) Three findings in 24 months, by contempt or entered agency determination, are a change in
  circumstances.

**2705.031(B)(2):** the agency may file contempt for the parent.

## PART SIX

**2919.23** ((A) through (D) unchanged; *Parra* intact):
- (E)(1) Withholding court-ordered time is keeping "without privilege," unless a lawful excuse
  applies.
- (E)(2) A reasonable reading of an unclear order is neither knowing nor reckless.
- (E)(3) It is not "a civil matter."
- (F) Referral from the agency or a court. Within 60 days (one 30-day extension), the prosecutor
  charges or sends a notice of declination. No reasons are needed; the declination is not
  reviewable; a later prosecution or a 2935.09 private complaint is not barred.

## PART SEVEN

- **Section 3:**
  - the presumption applies to allocations decided after the effective date;
  - existing orders stand;
  - the status motion is available;
  - existing restrictions run to the later of 36 months or 12 months after enactment;
  - the enforcement provisions apply to conduct after the effective date;
  - **the agency starts one year after enactment**.
- **Section 4:** child support calculation is unchanged.
- **Section 5:**
  - statewide forms (requested of the Supreme Court);
  - agency forms and funding (the Department of Job and Family Services; state appropriation plus
    42 U.S.C. 669b);
  - an annual data request.
- **Section 6:** an intent to conform to S.B. 174 if it is enacted.
