# PART I — SECTIONS ENACTED

## Sec. 3109.081. Short title.

Sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code may be cited as the "Ohio Equal
Parenting and Parent-Child Relationship Protection Act."

---

## Sec. 3109.082. Legislative policy.

It is the policy of this state that:

**(A)** A child ordinarily benefits from a continuing, meaningful relationship with both of
the child's parents.

**(B)** Each legal parent of a child has equal parental status, without regard to the
parent's sex or to whether the child's parents were ever married to each other.

**(C)** When a child's parents live separately, equal parenting time and shared parental
responsibility are presumed to be in the best interest of the child unless evidence
establishes that a different arrangement is necessary to protect the welfare of the child.

**(D)** The presumptions established by this Act yield to the protection of children and
parents. Nothing in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code diminishes the
obligation of a court to protect a child from abuse, neglect, abandonment, or substantial
danger, or to protect a parent from domestic violence.

**(E)** This section states the policy of the state. It does not create a cause of action
and does not confer a right enforceable apart from sections 3109.083 to 3109.0892 of the
Revised Code.

---

## Sec. 3109.083. Definitions.

As used in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code:

**(A)** "Equal parenting time" means approximately equal periods of physical parenting time
with each parent measured over a repeating cycle of not more than four weeks. Exact
mathematical equality is not required. A deviation that is attributable to the number of
days in a week or month, a school calendar, a holiday schedule, or reasonable travel does
not prevent a schedule from constituting equal parenting time.

**(B)** "Major decision" means a decision regarding the child's education, non-emergency
health care, or religious upbringing, or another decision having a long-term effect on the
child's welfare. "Major decision" does not include a decision regarding the child's
day-to-day care, routine activities, or ordinary discipline.

**(C)** "Parent" means a person whose parent and child relationship with the child is
established under Chapter 3107. or 3111. of the Revised Code or under section 3109.085 of
the Revised Code, or who is otherwise a legal parent of the child under the law of this
state.

**(D)** "Parenting time" has the same meaning as in **[A]** division (A)(21) of section
3109.04 of the Revised Code **[B]** sections 3109.04 and 3109.051 of the Revised Code, and
includes time during which a parent has responsibility for and physical care of the child.

**(E)** "Parenting-time interference" means conduct that prevents or substantially
interferes with the exercise of another parent's parenting time under a court order.

**(F)** The terms "abused child," "neglected child," and "domestic violence" have the same
meanings as in sections 2151.031, 2151.03, and 3113.31 of the Revised Code, respectively.

---

## Sec. 3109.084. Equal parental status; no preference; fit parents.

**(A)** Once two persons are established as the parents of the same child, neither parent
has parental rights superior to those of the other parent by reason of the parent's sex or
by reason of whether the parents were married to each other. Each parent stands upon an
equality with the other parent with respect to the child unless a court has entered an order
restricting the rights of one parent.

**(B)** In allocating **[A]** parental rights and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental
rights and responsibilities for the care of a child, or in ordering parenting time, a court
shall not give preference to a parent because of the parent's sex or because the parents were
or were not married to each other.

**(C)** There is a rebuttable presumption that a fit parent acts in the best interest of the
parent's child. A court shall give to the determination of a fit parent the special weight
required by the Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution and by Section 1 of
Article I of the Ohio Constitution. This division does not limit the authority of a court to
make any finding or order authorized by sections 3109.086 to 3109.0871 of the Revised Code
or by any other provision of the Revised Code.

---

## Sec. 3109.085. Parents who were never married to each other.

**(A) Equal parental status on establishment of parentage.** On and after the date on which
the parent and child relationship between a child and each of the child's parents is
established, each parent of the child has equal parental status under division (A) of section
3109.084 of the Revised Code. For purposes of this division, the parent and child
relationship is established when any of the following occurs:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** An acknowledgment of paternity becomes final under section 2151.232,
3111.25, or 3111.821 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A court or the department of job and family services determines the
existence of a parent and child relationship under Chapter 3111. of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** A parent and child relationship is established by adoption under Chapter
3107. of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** A woman gives birth to the child.

**(B) No separate action required.** No action, motion, complaint, or other separate
proceeding is required in order for a parent to obtain the parental status conferred by
division (A) of this section.

**(C) Legal status distinguished from schedule.** Division (A) of this section does not
itself establish a parenting time schedule or allocate **[A]** parental rights and parenting
responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and responsibilities for the care of the child.
Either parent may commence a proceeding to allocate **[A]** parental rights and parenting
responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and responsibilities for the care of the child or to
establish a parenting time schedule, and section 3109.086 of the Revised Code applies in that
proceeding.

**(D) Interim schedule.** Except as provided in division (E) of this section, if the parents
of a child reside separately at the time the parent and child relationship is established
under division (A) of this section and no court order specifying a parenting time schedule
for the child is in effect, the minimum parenting time schedule established by division (B)
of section 3109.087 of the Revised Code applies by operation of law until a court issues an
order specifying a parenting time schedule. Either parent may file a motion for a temporary
or permanent order specifying a different schedule, and section 3109.086 of the Revised Code
applies to that motion.

**(E) Exceptions to the interim schedule.** Division (D) of this section does not apply, and
a parent is not entitled to parenting time under that division, if any of the following
applies to that parent:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** A protection order, consent agreement, or temporary protection order
issued under section 2151.34, 2903.213, 2903.214, 2919.26, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code, or
a substantially similar order of a court of another state, restricts the parent's contact with
the child or with the child's other parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The parent has been convicted of or pleaded guilty to a violation of
section 2907.02 or 2907.03 of the Revised Code, the victim of which was the child's other
parent, and the child was conceived as a result of that violation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** A court finds, on the record, that the child faces an immediate
substantial risk to the child's physical safety if parenting time is exercised under division
(D) of this section. A court may make a finding under this division on an ex parte basis,
subject to a hearing under division (D) of section 3109.0872 of the Revised Code.

**(F) Rape and sexual battery.** A parent described in division (E)(2) of this section is not
entitled to the presumptions established by section 3109.086 of the Revised Code, is not
entitled to parenting time under section 3109.087 of the Revised Code, and shall not be
designated **[A]** the designated parent and legal custodian **[B]** the residential parent
and legal custodian of the child, unless the court determines that special circumstances
warrant a different result and states those circumstances in writing.

## Sec. 3109.086. Presumption of equal parenting time and shared decision-making.

**(A) Parenting time.** There is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the
evidence, that equal parenting time is in the best interest of the child.

**(B) Major decisions.** There is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the
evidence, that shared parental decision-making with respect to major decisions is in the best
interest of the child. All of the following apply to shared parental decision-making:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Each parent may make decisions regarding the child's day-to-day care
while the child is in that parent's physical care, without the agreement of the other parent.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Either parent may make any decision necessary to protect the child in an
emergency.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Shared parental decision-making does not require the parents to agree on
any matter other than a major decision, and does not itself require equal parenting time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** The presumptions established by divisions (A) and (B) of this section are
rebutted independently of each other. The rebuttal of one presumption does not rebut the
other.

**(C) Proceedings to which this section applies.** The presumptions established by this
section apply in any proceeding in which a court initially allocates **[A]** parental rights
and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and responsibilities for the care of a
child, including a divorce, dissolution of marriage, legal separation, or annulment
proceeding, a proceeding under section 2151.23, 3109.12, or 3111.13 of the Revised Code, and
a proceeding involving parents who were never married to each other, and apply to a temporary
order as provided in section 3109.0872 of the Revised Code. This section does not apply to a
motion to modify an order entered before the effective date of this section.

**(D) When the presumptions do not apply.** The presumptions established by this section do
not apply in any of the following circumstances:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The parents have agreed as to all matters relating to parenting time and
major decisions and the court approves the agreement;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** No party requests equal or substantially equal parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** As to a party against whom a protection order or consent agreement
described in division (E)(1) of section 3109.085 of the Revised Code is being or has been
entered on behalf of the other party or the child. In that circumstance the court shall weigh
all relevant factors in determining the best interest of the child.

**(E) Grounds on which the presumptions may be rebutted.** In determining whether a
presumption established by this section is rebutted, the court shall consider all relevant
evidence, including evidence of any of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Domestic violence committed by a parent against the other parent, the
child, or another member of either parent's household;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Physical or sexual abuse of the child or of another child by a parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Serious neglect of the child or of another child by a parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** Abandonment of the child by a parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** Any other conduct or condition of a parent that creates a substantial
danger to the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6)** A serious impairment of a parent, including impairment resulting from the
abuse of alcohol, a drug of abuse, or another substance, that affects the parent's ability to
care for the child safely;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(7)** The incarceration of a parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(8)** The distance between the parents' residences, or other geographic
circumstances, making equal parenting time impracticable;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(9)** That a parent, without just cause, has repeatedly interfered with the
other parent's exercise of court-ordered parenting time, or has engaged in intentional
interference with a parent-child relationship as defined in section 3109.088 of the Revised
Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(10)** Any other fact establishing that equal parenting time or shared parental
decision-making would materially harm the welfare of the child.

**(F) Insufficient grounds.** None of the following, standing alone, rebuts a presumption
established by this section:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The existence of conflict, disagreement, or animosity between the
parents;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A parent's objection to equal parenting time or to shared parental
decision-making;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** A parent's unwillingness to communicate or cooperate with the other
parent, where the unwillingness is that of the parent asserting it. A parent may not rebut a
presumption established by this section by relying on a condition that the parent's own
conduct created or maintained;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** The stated preference of the child, except as provided in section
3109.089 of the Revised Code.

**(G) Domestic violence; reverse presumption.** If the court finds that a party has committed
two or more acts of domestic violence against another party or against a child of either
party, there is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the evidence, that equal
parenting time and shared parental decision-making are **not** in the best interest of the
child as to that party.

**(H)** Nothing in this section limits the authority of a court to approve an agreement of
the parents, to order a restriction under section 3109.087 or 3109.0871 of the Revised Code,
or to act under Chapter 2151. or section 3113.31 of the Revised Code.

## Sec. 3109.0861. Findings required when a presumption is rebutted.

**(A)** If a court orders substantially less than equal parenting time over the objection of
a parent, the court shall state in writing both of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The facts that rebut the presumption established by division (A) of
section 3109.086 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Why the parenting time schedule ordered by the court is necessary for the
welfare of the child.

**(B)** If a court orders that major decisions be made other than jointly, over the objection
of a parent, the court shall state in writing the facts that rebut the presumption established
by division (B) of section 3109.086 of the Revised Code.

**(C)** Findings made under this section satisfy Civil Rule 52 with respect to the matters
stated, and no request by a party is necessary. Division (F)(1) of section 3109.051 of the
Revised Code does not limit this section.

**(D)** This section does not require the court to issue an opinion, to address each factor
relevant to the best interest of the child, to make findings in any particular form, or to
make findings of any particular length. A concise statement of the specific facts relied upon
is sufficient.

---

## Sec. 3109.0862. Domestic violence and abuse considered first.

**(A)** Before considering any other factor relevant to the best interest of the child, a
court allocating **[A]** parental rights and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental
rights and responsibilities for the care of a child or ordering parenting time shall do both
of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Consider any allegation of domestic violence, of an abused child, or of a
neglected child, committed by a party against another party or against a child of either
party;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Make written findings on the record as to the impact, if any, of an
allegation described in division (A)(1) of this section on the child.

**(B)** A finding that an allegation described in division (A)(1) of this section has no
impact on the child, or that the allegation was not established, is a sufficient finding under
division (A)(2) of this section.

**(C)** A court shall not consider against a party the party's willingness or ability to
facilitate or encourage the child's relationship with the other parent, or the likelihood that
the party will allow the child frequent, meaningful, and continuing contact with the other
parent, if the court finds both of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The other parent engaged in domestic violence against that party or
against a child of either party;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A continuing relationship with the other parent would endanger the health
or safety of that party or of the child.

---

## Sec. 3109.0863. Schedule maximizing each parent's time.

If a presumption established by division (A) of section 3109.086 of the Revised Code is
rebutted, or if equal parenting time is impracticable, the court shall, absent an agreement
between the parents as to all matters relating to parenting time, construct a parenting time
schedule that maximizes the time each parent has with the child and that is consistent with
ensuring the child's welfare, subject to section 3109.087 of the Revised Code.

---

## Sec. 3109.087. Minimum parenting time.

**(A) Floor.** Except as provided in division (C) of this section, a court shall not order a
parent less parenting time than the minimum parenting time schedule established by division
(B) of this section, and that schedule applies by operation of law where no court order
specifies a parenting time schedule for the child.

**(B) Minimum parenting time schedule.** The minimum parenting time schedule consists of all
of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** One weekday evening each week, on a day specified by the parent exercising
the parenting time or by the court, or on Wednesday if not specified, beginning at five-thirty
p.m. and ending at eight-thirty p.m., or, at the election of that parent, beginning when the
child's school is regularly dismissed and ending at eight-thirty p.m.;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Alternating weekends, beginning at six p.m. on Friday and ending at six
p.m. on Sunday, or, at the election of the parent exercising the parenting time, beginning when
the child's school is regularly dismissed on Friday and ending at six p.m. on Sunday;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Alternating holidays, allocated so that each parent has approximately half
of the holidays observed by the child's family in each calendar year and so that each holiday
alternates between the parents in successive years;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** Up to four weeks of extended parenting time each year when school is not
in session for summer break, of which not more than two weeks need be uninterrupted, on notice
to the other parent not later than the first day of May;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** Reasonable telephone and electronic contact between the child and each
parent during the other parent's parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6)** For a child under eighteen months of age, in place of divisions (B)(1),
(2), and (4) of this section, periods of parenting time that are shorter and more frequent than
those described in those divisions and that total not less than the time described in those
divisions over a repeating cycle of not more than four weeks.

**(C) Departure below the minimum.** A court may order less parenting time than the minimum
parenting time schedule, including supervised parenting time, parenting time in a protected
setting, or no parenting time, only if both of the following apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The court finds, based upon a preponderance of the evidence, either of the
following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(a)** That the restriction is reasonably calculated to protect the
child from physical, sexual, or emotional abuse or to protect a parent from domestic violence;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(b)** That one or more of the circumstances described in **[A]**
divisions (A) to (I) of section 3109.0456 of the Revised Code **[B]** division (I) of this
section applies, and that the minimum parenting time schedule would endanger the child's
physical or emotional welfare;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The court imposes the least restrictive arrangement reasonably necessary
to accomplish that protection and states in writing the facts supporting the finding under
division (C)(1) of this section and the reason the arrangement ordered is the least restrictive
arrangement reasonably necessary.

**(D) The minimum is a floor.** The minimum parenting time schedule established by division
(B) of this section is not the preferred parenting time schedule. No presumption arises in
favor of the minimum parenting time schedule, and an order awarding the minimum parenting time
schedule does not satisfy section 3109.086 or 3109.0863 of the Revised Code.

**(E) Local rules.** The general assembly requests that each court of common pleas conform the
standard parenting time guidelines adopted by rule under division (F)(2) of section 3109.051 of
the Revised Code to the minimum parenting time schedule established by division (B) of this
section. A court may adopt standard parenting time guidelines that provide more parenting time
than the minimum parenting time schedule. Regardless of the content of any rule adopted under
division (F)(2) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code, a court shall not order less parenting
time than the minimum parenting time schedule except as provided in division (C) of this
section.

**(F)** Nothing in this section limits an agreement of the parents that a court approves, or
an order issued under section 3109.086 or 3109.0863 of the Revised Code providing more
parenting time than the minimum parenting time schedule.

**(G) Graduated schedules.** If a parent has not exercised parenting time with the child for a
substantial period, the court may order a graduated parenting time schedule that reaches the
minimum parenting time schedule over a period stated in the order, not exceeding one year. An
order under this division is not a departure under division (C) of this section.

**(H) Agreement.** This section does not apply where the parents have agreed to a parenting time
schedule and the court approves the agreement.

**[B] (I) Grounds.** *(This division operates only under Track B. If S.B. 174 is enacted,
division (C)(1)(b) of this section instead cross-references section 3109.0456 of the Revised
Code and this division is omitted.)* The circumstances referred to in division (C)(1)(b) of this
section are all of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Willful neglect, or substantial nonperformance, of parental
responsibilities;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Long-term emotional or physical impairment that interferes with parental
responsibilities;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Impairment from alcohol, drug, or other substance abuse that interferes with
parental responsibilities;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** The absence or substantial impairment of ties between the parent and the
child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** Conduct that creates a danger to the child's psychological, social,
cognitive, emotional, or physical development;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6)** Denial of access of the other parent to the child for protracted periods of
time without justifiable cause;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(7)** Physical, sexual, or emotional abuse of the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(8)** An act of domestic violence as defined in section 3113.31 of the Revised
Code, a sexually oriented offense, or an assault that caused serious bodily injury or placed
another person in fear of imminent serious physical harm;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(9)** Knowingly consenting to the child being in the presence of a person who has
committed any act that would constitute the commission of a sexually oriented offense, an

child in this state.

## Sec. 3109.0871. Child safety.

**(A)** Nothing in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code requires a court to
order equal parenting time, shared parental decision-making, or the minimum parenting time
schedule if the court finds a substantial threat to the physical safety of the child or a
substantial threat to the safety of a parent from domestic violence.

**(B)** A finding under this section shall be based upon a preponderance of the evidence. A
conviction of or plea of guilty to any offense is **not** required for a finding under this
section, and a court may make a finding under this section upon credible evidence.

**(C)** None of the following, standing alone, rebuts a presumption established by section
3109.086 of the Revised Code, suspends the minimum parenting time schedule, or requires any
restriction:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** An allegation of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or criminal conduct;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A report to a public children services agency or to a law enforcement
agency;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The filing of a petition, motion, or complaint.

**(D)** Nothing in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code limits section 2151.34,
2903.213, 2903.214, 2919.26, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code, Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code,
or the authority of a juvenile court.

## Sec. 3109.0872. Temporary orders.

**(A)** Section 3109.086 of the Revised Code applies to a temporary order allocating
**[A]** parental rights and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and
responsibilities for the care of a child or ordering parenting time.

**(B)** If a temporary order materially departs from equal parenting time over the objection
of a parent, the court shall make the findings required by section 3109.0861 of the Revised
Code. No other findings are required.

**(C) No inference from a temporary order.** In allocating **[A]** parental rights and
parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and responsibilities for the care of a
child, a court shall not draw any presumption or inference from a temporary order. Neither the
existence nor the duration of a temporary order is a change in circumstances, and neither is
evidence of the child's adjustment to the child's home, school, or community.

**(D)** If a temporary order is issued without an oral hearing, a party may file a written
request for an oral hearing to modify the order. The court shall conduct an oral hearing not
later than twenty-eight days after the filing of the request unless good cause is shown.

**(E)** On the motion of a party filed more than twelve months after a temporary order is
issued, the court shall either conduct a final hearing on the allocation of **[A]** parental
rights and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and responsibilities for the care
of the child or state on the record the reason a final hearing cannot be conducted.

## Sec. 3109.088. Intentional interference with a parent-child relationship.

**(A) Definition.** "Intentional interference with a parent-child relationship" means a
knowing course of conduct, without lawful justification, that is intended to prevent or
substantially impair a child's relationship with the child's other parent under a court order.

**(B) Qualifying conduct.** Conduct that may constitute intentional interference with a
parent-child relationship includes any of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Intentionally denying the other parent court-ordered parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Intentionally preventing a scheduled exchange of the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Concealing the location of the child in order to prevent the other parent
from exercising lawful parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** Knowingly causing the other parent to lose a substantial portion of
court-ordered parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** Directing the child to refuse court-ordered parenting time, or knowingly
encouraging the child to refuse court-ordered parenting time, where the person also fails to make
reasonable efforts under division (A) of section 3109.089 of the Revised Code, and where the
person acts for the purpose of interfering with the child's relationship with the other parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6)** Knowingly making a materially false representation of fact for the purpose
of preventing the other parent from exercising lawful parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(7)** Knowingly making a materially false report of abuse, neglect, or criminal
conduct for the purpose of preventing the other parent from exercising lawful parenting time,
subject to section 3109.0882 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(8)** Intentionally withholding medical, educational, or location information
that the person is required by law or court order to provide, as part of a course of conduct
intended to substantially impair the child's relationship with the other parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(9)** Relocating the child, or concealing the child, in violation of a court
order or of a notice requirement imposed by law, for the purpose of preventing the other parent
from exercising lawful parenting time.

**(C) No psychological syndrome required or sufficient.** Nothing in this section requires
proof of any psychological syndrome, diagnosis, or condition, and proof of any psychological
syndrome, diagnosis, or condition is not sufficient to establish intentional interference with
a parent-child relationship. A finding under this section shall be based upon the conduct of
the person and the purpose with which the person acted.

**(D) Substantial interference.** Conduct constitutes substantial interference with a
parent-child relationship if either of the following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** A parent knowingly and without lawful justification causes the other parent
to lose three or more periods of court-ordered parenting time within any ninety-day period;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A parent knowingly and without lawful justification causes the other parent
to lose at least fifty per cent of that parent's court-ordered parenting time within any
thirty-day period.

**(E)** Division (D) of this section defines a term. It does not establish a presumption, and
it does not relieve a party or the state of the burden of proving any element of a claim or
offense.

**(F)** A finding of intentional interference with a parent-child relationship is a ground on
which a presumption established by section 3109.086 of the Revised Code may be rebutted, is a
factor relevant to the best interest of the child, and is a change in circumstances for
purposes of **[A]** section 3109.0419 **[B]** division (E)(1)(a) of section 3109.04 of the
Revised Code.

**(G) Expression protected.** Nothing in this section prohibits, or imposes liability for, a
parent's expression of opinion, criticism, or negative statements regarding the other parent, or
a parent's truthful statement to the child or to any other person. This section does not
authorize a court to prohibit a parent from speaking about the other parent.

## Sec. 3109.0881. Expedited enforcement of parenting time.

**(A) Motion.** A parent may file a motion alleging that the other parent has denied or
interfered with the movant's court-ordered parenting time. The motion shall state all of the
following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The date of the order awarding the parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A description of the parenting time awarded;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The dates on which parenting time was denied or interfered with and the
amount of parenting time lost;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** A description of any efforts made to exercise the parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** The relief requested.

**(B) Hearing.** On the filing of a motion under this section, the court shall hold an initial
hearing not later than twenty-eight days after service of the motion unless good cause is
shown. At any time before ruling on the motion the court may issue a temporary order necessary
to protect the relationship between the child and either parent.

**(C) Proof.** Proof of the order awarding parenting time and of the parenting time that was
not exercised establishes a prima facie case that parenting time was denied. On that showing,
the burden of producing evidence of lawful justification for the denial is on the responding
parent. The burden of persuasion remains on the movant.

**(D) Consequences.** If the court finds that a parent denied or interfered with court-ordered
parenting time without lawful justification, all of the following apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1) First finding.** The court shall order make-up parenting time of the same
type and duration as the parenting time that was denied, to be exercised not later than six
months after the order, and shall notify the parent in writing of the consequences of a further
violation. The court may order any other remedy authorized by law.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2) Second and subsequent findings.** In addition to make-up parenting time under
division (D)(1) of this section, the court shall order at least one of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(a)** Payment of the reasonable costs and attorney's fees of the
other parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(b)** A civil sanction of not more than five hundred dollars for
each period of parenting time denied;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(c)** Completion of parent education at the expense of the parent who
denied the parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(d)** Exchange of the child in a protected setting or with a neutral
party;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(e)** Posting of a bond, in cash or with sufficient sureties,
conditioned on compliance with the order awarding parenting time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3) Three or more findings.** If the court finds that a parent has been found on
three or more occasions to have denied or interfered with court-ordered parenting time without
lawful justification, all of the following apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(a)** The make-up parenting time ordered shall be, in total, twice
the duration of the parenting time that was denied;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(b)** The court shall not waive the requirement that the parent pay
the reasonable costs and attorney's fees of the other parent;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(c)** The pattern of findings constitutes a change in circumstances
for purposes of **[A]** section 3109.0419 **[B]** division (E)(1)(a) of section 3109.04 of the
Revised Code, and the court shall consider whether modification of the allocation of
**[A]** parental rights and parenting responsibilities **[B]** parental rights and
responsibilities for the care of the child is necessary to serve the best interest of the child.

**(E) De minimis and agreed changes.** A court shall not impose a consequence under division
(D)(2) or (D)(3) of this section, and may decline to find a violation, if the denial of
parenting time was inadvertent, was of minimal duration, was promptly cured, or occurred with
the agreement of the other parent. Make-up parenting time under division (D)(1) of this section
remains available.

**(F) Remedies cumulative.** This section does not limit division (K) of section 3109.051,
section 2705.031, or **[A]** sections 3109.0491 to 3109.0493 of the Revised Code. The remedies
available under this section are in addition to and not in place of any other remedy.

## Sec. 3109.0882. Good-faith reports; false reports.

**(A) Protection for good-faith reports.** A report of suspected child abuse or child neglect,
or of suspected criminal conduct, that is made in good faith is not intentional interference
with a parent-child relationship under section 3109.088 of the Revised Code, is not a violation
of section 2919.232 of the Revised Code, and shall not be considered against the person who made
the report in determining the best interest of the child.

**(B)** A report is not intentional interference with a parent-child relationship and is not a
violation of section 2919.232 of the Revised Code merely because the report was unsubstantiated,
was not prosecuted, was not proven, or was mistaken.

**(C) Elements of a false report.** A report or statement may constitute conduct described in
division (B)(6) or (B)(7) of section 3109.088 of the Revised Code only if all of the following
are established:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The report or statement was materially false as to a matter of fact;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The person who made it knew that it was false;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The person made it for the purpose of preventing the other parent from
exercising lawful parenting time or of substantially impairing the child's relationship with the
other parent.

**(D)** Nothing in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code limits section 2151.421
of the Revised Code, including the duty to report imposed by that section and the immunity
conferred by that section.

**(E) Pending report.** A parent who declines to permit parenting time while a report described
in division (A) of this section is pending has lawful justification for purposes of sections
3109.0881 and 2919.232 of the Revised Code if the parent made the report promptly and in good
faith and complies with the direction of the public children services agency, the law enforcement
agency, or the court.

## Sec. 3109.089. Child's refusal; duty to facilitate.

**(A)** A parent shall make reasonable efforts to ensure that the child is available for and
complies with court-ordered parenting time.

**(B)** A child's refusal to exercise court-ordered parenting time is not lawful justification
for the denial of parenting time unless the parent made reasonable efforts under division (A) of
this section and promptly notified the other parent of the child's refusal.

**(C)** Reasonable efforts under division (A) of this section do not require a parent to use
physical force.

**(D)** Nothing in this section limits the authority of a court to consider the wishes and
concerns of a child of sufficient age and maturity under **[A]** division (A)(2) of section
3109.0430 and sections 3109.0445 to 3109.0451 **[B]** division (B) of section 3109.04 and
division (C) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(E)** If a child repeatedly refuses to exercise court-ordered parenting time, the court may,
on its own motion, inquire into the cause of the refusal.

**(F) Safe harbor.** A parent satisfies division (A) of this section by doing all of the
following: making the child available at the time and place required by the order; informing the
child of the scheduled parenting time; not discouraging the child from exercising it; and
promptly notifying the other parent if the child refuses. Division (A) of this section does not
require a parent to use physical force, to make any particular statement, or to express any
particular view.

## Sec. 3109.0891. Relocation.

**(A)** A parent shall not relocate the parent's residence or the child's residence primarily
for the purpose of defeating the other parent's exercise of parenting time or of substantially
impairing the child's relationship with the other parent. In any proceeding relating to a
relocation, the court shall consider whether a relocation was undertaken or is proposed for that
purpose.

**(B)** If the distance between the parents' residences or other geographic circumstances make
equal parenting time impracticable, the court shall construct a schedule under section 3109.0863
of the Revised Code that maximizes each parent's parenting time, including through extended
blocks of parenting time, school breaks, summer parenting time, holidays, and reasonable
telephone and electronic contact, and shall allocate responsibility for transportation and its
cost.

**(C)** Nothing in sections 3109.081 to 3109.0892 of the Revised Code requires a court to order
equal parenting time where geographic circumstances make equal parenting time impracticable.

**(D)** This section does not limit **[A]** sections 3109.0470 to 3109.0479 **[B]** division (G)
of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

## Sec. 3109.0892. Access to records and information.

Unless a court order specifically provides otherwise, each parent of a child has equal and
direct access to the child's school records, health records, records of a child care provider,
information regarding the child's extracurricular activities and schedules, and emergency
information regarding the child. A school, health care provider, or child care provider shall
not treat one parent as subordinate to the other parent with respect to that access because the
parents reside separately or because the parents were never married to each other. This section
does not limit **[A]** sections 3109.0516 to 3109.0529 **[B]** divisions (H) to (J) of section
3109.051, section 3109.11, section 3319.321, or section 5104.039 of the Revised Code.

## Sec. 2919.232. Interference with parenting time.

**(A) Offense.** No person having custody or control of a child shall knowingly and without
lawful justification engage in a course of conduct that substantially interferes with another
parent's exercise of parenting time under a court order or with another parent's legally
established relationship with the child.

**(B) Course of conduct.** For purposes of division (A) of this section, a person engages in a
course of conduct that substantially interferes only if either of the following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The person, on three or more occasions within any ninety-day period,
knowingly and without lawful justification causes the other parent to lose a period of
court-ordered parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The person knowingly and without lawful justification deprives the other
parent of court-ordered parenting time for thirty or more consecutive days.

**(C) Lawful justification.** The state shall prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the person
acted without lawful justification. A person acts with lawful justification if any of the
following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The person acted in compliance with another valid court order, including a
protection order or consent agreement;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The person reasonably believed that the person's conduct was necessary to
prevent imminent physical harm to the child or to the person;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The person acted in good faith in response to suspected child abuse or
child neglect in accordance with section 3109.0882 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** The person's conduct was necessary because of a medical emergency;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** The person's conduct resulted from a circumstance beyond the person's
control, including the illness of the child or of the person, a weather emergency, or a failure
of transportation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(6)** The other parent agreed to the conduct, except that the other parent's
agreement to a parenting time schedule generally is not agreement to a particular denial of
parenting time or to concealment of the child.

**(D) Conduct not prohibited.** No person shall be convicted under this section on the basis of
an accident, a misunderstanding, a change in a parenting time schedule to which both parents
agreed, the illness of the child or of the person, a weather emergency, another circumstance
outside the person's control, or a reasonable good-faith action taken to protect the child.

**(E) Other remedies do not preclude prosecution.** The availability of contempt, modification
of an order, or any other civil remedy does not preclude the investigation or prosecution of a
violation of this section.

**(F) Penalties.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Whoever violates this section is guilty of interference with parenting time.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Except as otherwise provided in division (F)(3) of this section,
interference with parenting time is a misdemeanor of the first degree.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Interference with parenting time is a felony of the fifth degree if any of
the following applies:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(a)** The offender previously has been convicted of or pleaded guilty
to a violation of this section or of section 2919.23 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(b)** The offender engaged in the conduct after a court found that the
offender had engaged in intentional interference with a parent-child relationship under section
3109.088 of the Revised Code;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(c)** The offender concealed the location of the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(d)** The offender removed the child from this state in order to
defeat the other parent's exercise of parenting time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(e)** The offender threatened or used force against the other parent
or the child in the course of the conduct;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(f)** The conduct deprived the other parent of court-ordered
parenting time for more than ninety consecutive days.

**(G) Exclusions.** This section does not apply to conduct authorized or required by a
protection order, a consent agreement, an order of a juvenile court, or the direction of a
public children services agency acting within its authority.

**(H)** This section does not limit section 2919.23 of the Revised Code. Conduct that violates
both this section and section 2919.23 of the Revised Code may be prosecuted under either
section.

**(I) Expression not a basis for conviction.** No person shall be convicted under this section
on the basis of conduct described in division (B)(5), (B)(6), or (B)(7) of section 3109.088 of
the Revised Code alone.

**(J) Burden; no presumed element.** The state shall prove each element of this offense beyond a
reasonable doubt. No element of this offense, including the offender's knowledge or purpose, may
be presumed or inferred as a matter of law from proof of the number or duration of periods of
parenting time denied.

**(K) Allied offenses.** If the same conduct constitutes a violation of this section and of
section 2919.23 of the Revised Code, the offender may be convicted of only one of those
offenses.

**(L) Order must be definite.** No person shall be convicted under this section unless the
provision of the court order that the person is alleged to have violated is definite and
specific as to the periods of parenting time allocated. A person does not violate this section
by acting on a reasonable interpretation of an ambiguous order.

# APPENDIX TO DOCUMENT 4 — OPEN DRAFTING QUESTIONS FOR THE SPONSOR

These are genuine choices, not oversights. Each is flagged because a sponsor, not a drafter,
should make it.

**1. Should the reverse presumption in § 3109.086(G) require two acts, or one?**
Kentucky uses two (KRS 403.315(2)). One act of domestic violence is already a rebuttal ground
under § 3109.086(E)(1); the question is only whether it should flip the burden. Two is the enacted
model and the more defensible starting position. A sponsor who wants the stronger victim
protection can use one, at some cost to the presumption's reach.

**2. Should the criminal threshold in § 2919.232(B)(1) be three denials in 90 days, or should the
offence instead be indexed to prior civil findings, as Texas does?**
Indexing to prior findings under § 3109.0881 is cleaner — it is self-proving from the docket, it
guarantees the parent had notice and an opportunity to comply, and it makes the criminal track a
genuine last resort. Its drawback is that it makes prosecution impossible where no civil motion was
ever filed, which is exactly the situation of a parent who cannot afford counsel. The draft uses the
denial count for that reason, but a sponsor who wants maximum defensibility should consider adding
"or has previously been found under section 3109.0881 to have denied parenting time without lawful
justification on two or more occasions" as an **alternative** route in (B), and note that the felony
enhancement in (F)(3)(b) already uses the indexed approach.

**3. Should § 3109.087(B) specify hours, or delegate to a Supreme Court form?**
The draft specifies hours, on the Utah model, because a delegated schedule is a schedule that can be
changed without a vote. The cost is that any future adjustment needs legislation. An alternative is
to state the components in statute — one weekday evening, alternating weekends, alternating holidays,
four weeks summer, electronic contact — and let the Supreme Court of Ohio prescribe times by uniform
form under its Rules of Superintendence. That version is shorter and more flexible and would likely
draw less judicial opposition.

**4. Should the Act reach modifications at all?**
As drafted it does not, except for enforcement. That is the concession to the floodgate objection.
A sponsor who wants existing orders reached has two options short of full retroactivity: a limited
window (for example, 24 months) in which a parent may seek a parenting-time-only modification under
the best-interest standard without showing a change in circumstances; or a provision making the Act
applicable on the next modification motion filed for any other reason. Both would draw the Ohio
State Bar Association's opposition in the terms it used in 2023, and both should be priced
accordingly.

**5. Should § 3109.084(C) be included?**
It codifies *Troxel*. It changes no outcome the Constitution does not already require. National
Parents Organization asked for it; the S.B. 174 sponsors declined to add it. Including it is
principled and low-risk, but a sponsor should know that it is the one provision in this draft that
the S.B. 174 coalition has already publicly resisted.

**6. Age of the child under § 3109.087(B)(6).**
The draft uses eighteen months, following the Franklin County infant schedule. Utah draws its line
at five years (§ 30-3-35.5 for children under five). A sponsor may prefer a graduated set of tiers,
at the cost of length.
