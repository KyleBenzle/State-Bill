### Three things to read first

1. **S.B. 174 does not contain an equal-parenting presumption.** It requires courts to
   "maximize parenting time with each parent when it is in the best interest of the
   child" (proposed R.C. 3109.044) and lets a court deny a request for substantially
   equal parenting time on best-interest, safety, or "other good cause" grounds with
   written findings (proposed R.C. 3109.046, 3109.047). The As Passed by the Senate
   version also grants the court "complete discretion over the approval of a parenting
   plan" (proposed R.C. 3109.0412). This proposal supplies the missing baseline.

2. **S.B. 174 keeps the unmarried-mother sole-custodian rule.** Proposed R.C. 3109.0424
   carries current R.C. 3109.042(A) forward almost verbatim. So did H.B. 14. This
   proposal replaces it.

3. **The politics are the opposite of H.B. 14's.** S.B. 174 is supported by the Ohio
   Domestic Violence Network, the Ohio State Bar Association, the Ohio Judicial
   Conference, and the Ohio Association of Domestic Relations Judges — the groups that
   killed H.B. 14 — and is opposed by National Parents Organization. This draft is
   therefore written as an overlay on S.B. 174's architecture and terminology, not a
   competing rewrite of Chapter 3109.

### Standing drafting choices made in this proposal

- **Preponderance of the evidence**, not clear and convincing, to rebut the presumption.
  H.B. 14's clear-and-convincing standard was the single most effective objection
  against it, and every enacted 50/50 presumption in the country uses preponderance.
- **No automatic reopening of existing orders.** Enactment alone is not a change in
  circumstances. This answers the "floodgate" objection directly.
- **No psychological syndrome anywhere in the bill.** Liability attaches to observable
  conduct only.
- **Criminal liability is narrow**: a knowing, repeated course of conduct, with a
  three-denials-in-90-days trigger, full safe harbors, and protection for good-faith
  abuse reports.
