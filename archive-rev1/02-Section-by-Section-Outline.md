# DOCUMENT 2 — SECTION-BY-SECTION OUTLINE

## OHIO EQUAL PARENTING AND PARENT-CHILD RELATIONSHIP PROTECTION ACT

**Short title.** R.C. 3109.081 designates the Act.
**Total operative length.** Eleven new Revised Code sections, four amended sections, one
repealed section. For comparison, S.B. 174 as passed by the Senate enacts about 150 new
sections and amends 105.

---

## How to read this outline

The Act is drafted to sit inside S.B. 174's framework. Column "Track A" is the provision
as it operates if S.B. 174 is enacted; column "Track B" is the same policy expressed
against current law if S.B. 174 dies. **The substantive rules are identical in both
tracks.** Only cross-references and vocabulary change, and the contingency section in
Part 5 does the switching automatically.

Section numbers in the 3109.081–3109.089 block were chosen because that block is
unoccupied in current Chapter 3109 and untouched by S.B. 174, which consumes
3109.041–3109.0570. **Final numbering is for the Legislative Service Commission to
assign.** Nothing in the policy depends on it.

## PART 1 — POLICY AND DEFINITIONS

### § 3109.081 — Short title
One sentence. No operative effect.

### § 3109.082 — Legislative policy
Four declarative divisions:

- **(A)** A child ordinarily benefits from a continuing, meaningful relationship with
  both parents.
- **(B)** Each legal parent has equal parental status, regardless of sex or whether the
  parents were ever married to each other.
- **(C)** When parents live separately, equal parenting time and shared parental
  responsibility are presumed to be in the child's best interest unless evidence
  establishes that a different arrangement is necessary to protect the child's welfare.
- **(D)** Nothing in the Act diminishes the state's obligation to protect children and
  parents from abuse, domestic violence, neglect, abandonment, or substantial danger,
  and the presumption yields to that obligation.

*Drafting notes.* Division (C) is the brief's core-principle sentence, edited to statutory
register. Division (D) is placed in the policy section deliberately so that every later
provision is read in its light — this is the structural answer to the "parent-entitlement
model" objection. The section states a policy; it creates no cause of action, and says so.

### § 3109.083 — Definitions
Five terms only. Every other term is borrowed from R.C. 3109.04 (as S.B. 174 would amend
it) or from current law.

| Term | Substance |
|---|---|
| "Equal parenting time" | Approximately equal physical parenting time over a repeating cycle; exact mathematical equality is not required and a deviation attributable to the calendar, school schedule, or travel does not defeat it |
| "Parent" | A person whose legal parentage is established under Ohio law; cross-reference only, no new substance |
| "Parenting time" | Cross-reference to S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.04(A)(21) / current usage |
| "Parenting-time interference" | Conduct that prevents or substantially interferes with another parent's parenting time under a court order |
| "Major decision" | Education, non-emergency health care, and religious upbringing subject to constitutional limits; expressly excludes day-to-day decisions |

*Deliberately not defined:* "parental alienation" and every cognate. The term appears
nowhere in the Act. The regulated conduct is named "intentional interference with a
parent-child relationship" and is defined by acts, in § 3109.088.

## PART 2 — EQUAL PARENTAL STATUS AND UNMARRIED PARENTS

### § 3109.084 — Equal parental status
- **(A)** Once two persons are legal parents of the same child, neither has superior
  parental rights by reason of sex or of marital status, and each stands on equal footing
  unless a court has entered an order restricting one parent's rights.
- **(B)** A court shall not prefer one parent because of the parent's sex or because the
  parents were or were not married to each other. *(Part XXII — one sentence. Deliberately
  narrower than S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0430(B), which covers gender and financial
  status; that provision is left in place and this one adds marital status.)*
- **(C)** Codifies the *Troxel* fit-parent presumption: there is a rebuttable presumption
  that a fit parent acts in the child's best interest, and a court shall give a fit
  parent's determination the special weight required by the United States Constitution.
  *(Answers the undefined-"special weight" defect in the pending House substitute; see
  Document 1 § C.4 and Document 6 § 1.)*

### § 3109.085 — Parents who were never married to each other
This is the section that replaces the rule in current R.C. 3109.042(A) and in S.B. 174
proposed R.C. 3109.0424.

- **(A) Legal status.** On final establishment of parentage — acknowledgment that has
  become final under R.C. 3111.25, 2151.232 or 3111.821, adjudication under Chapter 3111,
  or adoption — both legal parents have equal parental status under § 3109.084. **No
  separate action is required to obtain that status.**
- **(B) The practical schedule is a separate question.** Subsection (A) does not itself fix
  a parenting-time schedule. Either parent may commence a proceeding to allocate, and the
  presumption in § 3109.086 applies in that proceeding.
- **(C) Interim schedule.** Where the parents already live separately when parentage
  becomes final and no court order allocating parenting time is in effect, the minimum
  schedule in § 3109.087 applies by operation of law until a court order issues. Either
  parent may move for a different temporary schedule, and § 3109.086 governs that motion.
- **(D) Exceptions to (C).** The interim schedule does not apply, and a parent has no
  interim entitlement under it, if any of the following is true: a protection order or
  consent agreement under R.C. 2919.26, 3113.31 or 2151.34 restricts that parent's contact
  with the child or the other parent; the parent has been convicted of or pleaded guilty to
  rape or sexual battery of the other parent and the child was conceived as a result; or a
  court finds, on the record, that the child faces an immediate substantial risk to the
  child's physical safety.
- **(E)** The exclusion in current R.C. 3109.042(B) / S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0426 is
  preserved and made sex-neutral: a parent convicted of rape or sexual battery of the other
  parent, where the child was conceived as a result, is not entitled to the presumption or
  the interim schedule.

*Drafting notes.* The status/schedule split in (A)–(C) is the heart of this Part and the
answer to the brief's instruction to distinguish them. (A) costs the state nothing and
ends the anomaly that establishing paternity establishes an obligation but not a
relationship. (C) is what gives (A) practical content, and it is drafted as a *floor*, not
a 50/50 default, precisely because the parents' circumstances are unknown to any court at
that moment. Applying a 50/50 default by operation of law to parents who may never have
lived together and about whom no court has made any finding would be indefensible; applying
the least generous schedule any Ohio county publishes, subject to four safety exceptions
and immediately displaceable by motion, is not.

## PART 3 — THE PRESUMPTION AND WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT IS REBUTTED

### § 3109.086 — Presumption of equal parenting time and shared decision-making
The operative core. Seven divisions, each one rule.

- **(A) The presumption.** "There is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the
  evidence, that equal parenting time is in the best interest of the child." *(W.Va. Code
  § 48-9-102a, sentence one, verbatim in substance.)*
- **(B) Decision-making.** A separate, parallel presumption that the parents share major
  decisions. Each parent independently makes day-to-day decisions while the child is in
  that parent's care, and either parent may make emergency decisions. **Shared decision-
  making does not require agreement on day-to-day matters and does not itself require
  equal parenting time**, and the two presumptions are rebutted independently.
  *(A.R.S. § 25-403.02; H.B. 14 proposed R.C. 3109.0424(B) and 3109.0425(B).)*
- **(C) Scope.** The presumptions apply in every proceeding that initially allocates
  parenting responsibility — divorce, dissolution, legal separation, annulment, and
  proceedings involving parents who were never married — and to **temporary orders**. They
  do not apply to a motion to modify an order entered before the Act's effective date.
- **(D) Non-application.** The presumptions do not apply: where the parents have agreed on
  all matters relating to parenting time and the court approves the agreement; where no
  party requests equal or substantially equal parenting time; or as against a party
  protected by a domestic violence order, as to the party against whom it was entered.
  *(Ark. Code § 9-13-101; KRS 403.315(1).)*
- **(E) Rebuttal grounds.** A non-exclusive list, drawn tightly: domestic violence;
  physical or sexual abuse; serious neglect; abandonment; substantial danger to the child;
  serious impairment — including impairment from substance abuse — affecting the parent's
  ability to care for the child safely; incarceration; geographic distance making equal
  parenting time impracticable; and any other fact establishing that equal parenting time
  would materially harm the child's welfare.
- **(F) What does not rebut.** Four express negatives:
  1. the existence of conflict between the parents, standing alone;
  2. a parent's objection to equal parenting time, standing alone;
  3. a parent's unwillingness to cooperate, where that unwillingness is the objecting
     parent's own conduct — **a parent may not rely on conditions that parent created**;
  4. the child's stated preference, standing alone, except as § 3109.089 provides.
- **(G) Reverse presumption on domestic violence.** Where the court finds a party has
  committed two or more acts of domestic violence against another party or a child, there
  is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the evidence, that equal parenting
  time and shared decision-making are **not** in the child's best interest as to that
  party. *(KRS 403.315(2).)*

*Drafting notes.* (F)(3) is the anti-manufacture clause the brief asks for. It is drafted
as a limit on what evidence can *carry* a rebuttal rather than as a penalty, which keeps it
out of due-process trouble. Its lineage is NRS 125C.0025's frustrated-efforts clause and
H.B. 14 proposed R.C. 3109.0422(G). (G) is the provision most likely to bring
domestic-violence organisations to neutrality; it is enacted Kentucky law, not a drafting
invention.

### § 3109.0861 — Required findings when the presumption is rebutted
Two items, and no more.

If the court orders substantially less than equal parenting time over a parent's objection,
it shall state in writing:
1. the facts that rebut the presumption; and
2. why the ordered schedule is necessary for the child's welfare.

Plus: **(B)** a statement that findings under this section satisfy Civil Rule 52 as to the
parenting-time allocation and that no separate request is required; and **(C)** an express
statement that nothing in the section requires an opinion, a recitation of each
best-interest factor, or findings in any particular form.

*Drafting notes.* (C) exists because the judicial objection to H.B. 14 was workload. Two
findings is less paperwork than R.C. 3109.051(F)(1) already requires on request, and far
less than Fla. Stat. § 61.13(2)(c) requires (specific written findings on *all* factors in
every non-agreed case). Borrowed from W.Va. Code § 48-9-102a's economy rather than
Florida's.

### § 3109.0862 — Domestic violence and abuse considered first
Before reaching any other best-interest factor, the court shall consider any allegation of
domestic violence, child abuse, or child neglect committed by a party against another party
or a child of the parties, and make written findings on the record as to the impact, if
any, of the allegation on the child. A finding of no impact is a sufficient finding.

Also: the willingness of a party to facilitate the child's relationship with the other
parent shall **not** be considered against a party where the court finds the other parent
committed domestic violence against that party or a child and continuing contact would
endanger the health or safety of that party or the child.
*(KRS 403.270(3) and (4)(l), both effective July 15, 2026.)*

*Drafting notes.* This section costs the presumption nothing and is the single most
effective available answer to the "chilling effect on victims" objection, because it
requires the court to engage the allegation before anything else rather than treating it as
one of twenty-six factors. The second paragraph is the anti-weaponization clause; without
it, § 3109.086(F)(3) could be turned against a protective parent.

### § 3109.0863 — Maximum-time fallback
"If the presumption in division (A) of section 3109.086 is rebutted, or if equal parenting
time is impracticable, the court shall, absent an agreement of the parents as to all
matters relating to parenting time, construct a parenting time schedule that maximizes the
time each parent has with the child and is consistent with ensuring the child's welfare,
subject to section 3109.087."

*(W.Va. Code § 48-9-102a, sentence two, verbatim in substance; KRS 403.270(2), sentence
three; Divorce Act (Canada) s. 16(6).)*

*Drafting notes.* This is the provision that prevents "50/50 rejected → every other
weekend." One sentence. The "subject to section 3109.087" tail connects it to the floor.

## PART 4 — THE FLOOR, SAFETY, AND TEMPORARY ORDERS

### § 3109.087 — Minimum parenting time
- **(A) The floor.** Unless division (C) applies, a court shall not order a fit parent less
  parenting time than the minimum schedule, and the minimum schedule applies by operation
  of law where no court order specifies a schedule.
- **(B) Content of the minimum schedule.** Specified in the statute, at the level of the
  least generous schedule published by Ohio courts today:
  - one weekday evening each week;
  - alternating weekends from Friday evening to Sunday evening;
  - an alternating holiday rotation;
  - up to four weeks of extended parenting time when school is not in session for summer
    break, of which two weeks may be uninterrupted;
  - reasonable telephone and electronic contact; and
  - an age adjustment for a child under eighteen months, substituting shorter and more
    frequent periods.
  *(Structure from Utah Code § 30-3-35(3), (4); content calibrated to Franklin County
  Local Domestic Rule 27.1 / Local Juvenile Rule 22.1, Option D.)*
- **(C) Departure below the floor.** Permitted only if the court finds, by a preponderance
  of the evidence, that a restriction is reasonably calculated to protect the child from
  physical, sexual, or emotional abuse or a parent from domestic violence, and imposes the
  **least restrictive** arrangement reasonably necessary. The available restrictions are
  those in S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0457 (Track A) or a list carried into
  R.C. 3109.051 (Track B). Written findings required.
  *(S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0455 verbatim standard; "least restrictive means" from the
  pending House substitute.)*
- **(D) The floor is a floor.** Express statement that the minimum schedule is not the
  preferred schedule, creates no presumption in favour of itself, and shall not be treated
  as satisfying § 3109.086 or § 3109.0863.
- **(E) Local rules.** Each court of common pleas shall conform the standard parenting time
  guidelines adopted under R.C. 3109.051(F)(2) to the minimum schedule, and may adopt more
  generous guidelines. A local rule may not authorise less.
  *(The H.B. 508 device, operating through the existing R.C. 3109.051(F)(2) mandate.)*

*Drafting notes on (D).* This division does the most work per word in the entire Act. The
predictable failure mode of any statutory minimum is that it becomes the ceiling — judges
and practitioners drift to the number the legislature wrote down. (D) forecloses that
reading, and the three-step sequence in Document 3's flowchart reinforces it.

### § 3109.0871 — Child-safety exception
- **(A)** Nothing in the Act requires equal parenting time, shared decision-making, or the
  minimum schedule where the court finds a substantial threat to the child's physical
  safety or to a parent's safety from domestic violence.
- **(B)** The standard is preponderance of the evidence, and **a criminal conviction is not
  required.** A court may act on credible evidence.
- **(C)** An allegation, a report to a public children services agency, or the filing of a
  petition does not by itself rebut any presumption or suspend the minimum schedule.
- **(D)** Nothing limits R.C. 3113.31, R.C. 2151.34, R.C. 2919.26, Chapter 2151, or a
  juvenile court's authority.

*Drafting notes.* (B) and (C) are the two halves of the brief's instruction not to draft an
exception so broad that allegations are self-executing, while not requiring a conviction.
(B) is the concession to victim advocates; (C) is the concession to falsely accused
parents. Both are necessary and they are not in tension: (C) addresses the *automatic*
effect of a bare allegation; (B) addresses what a court may do once it weighs evidence.

### § 3109.0872 — Temporary orders
- **(A)** § 3109.086 applies to a temporary order allocating parenting time.
- **(B)** A temporary order that materially departs from equal parenting time requires the
  same two findings as § 3109.0861, and no more.
- **(C)** **No presumption from the passage of time.** The court shall draw no inference
  from a temporary order in making a final allocation, and the duration of a temporary
  order is not a change in circumstances and not evidence of the child's adjustment.
  *(S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0411, strengthened.)*
- **(D)** Where a temporary order is issued without an oral hearing, a party may request
  one and the court shall hold it not later than 28 days after the request unless good
  cause is shown. *(S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0423, adopted unchanged.)*
- **(E)** On a party's motion filed more than 12 months after a temporary order issues, the
  court shall either conduct a final hearing or state on the record why it cannot.

*Drafting notes.* (C) and (E) together are the answer to the brief's instruction to prevent
temporary orders from becoming permanent by inertia. (C) is the legal half; (E) is the
practical half, and (E) is drafted as a duty to act or explain rather than a deadline,
because a hard deadline would be unenforceable and would draw judicial opposition.

## PART 5 — ENFORCEMENT, INTERFERENCE, AND THE CRIMINAL OFFENCE

### § 3109.088 — Intentional interference with a parent-child relationship
- **(A) Definition.** "A knowing course of conduct, without lawful justification, intended
  to prevent or substantially impair a child's relationship with the child's other parent
  under a court order."
- **(B) Qualifying acts.** Nine, each an observable act paired with the required mental
  state:
  1. intentionally denying court-ordered parenting time;
  2. intentionally preventing a scheduled exchange;
  3. concealing the child's location to prevent lawful parenting time;
  4. knowingly causing the other parent to lose a substantial portion of court-ordered
     parenting time;
  5. knowingly encouraging or directing a child to refuse court-ordered parenting time for
     the purpose of interfering with the parent-child relationship;
  6. knowingly making a materially false statement of fact for the purpose of preventing
     lawful parenting time;
  7. knowingly making a materially false report of abuse or criminal conduct for the
     purpose of preventing lawful parenting time — **subject to § 3109.0882**;
  8. intentionally withholding legally required medical, educational, or location
     information as part of a course of conduct intended to substantially impair the
     parent-child relationship; and
  9. relocating or concealing the child in violation of a court order or a statutory notice
     requirement for the purpose of preventing lawful parenting time.
- **(C)** The phrase "parental alienation" is not used, and nothing in the section requires
  proof of any psychological syndrome, diagnosis, or condition. Expert psychological
  testimony is not required and is not sufficient.
- **(D) Substantial interference — the bright line.** Conduct constitutes substantial
  interference if either:
  - **(1)** a parent knowingly and without lawful justification causes the other parent to
    lose **three or more** periods of court-ordered parenting time within any **90-day**
    period; or
  - **(2)** a parent knowingly and without lawful justification causes the other parent to
    lose **at least 50 per cent** of that parent's court-ordered parenting time within any
    **30-day** period.

*Drafting notes on (C).* This division is the Brazil lesson, stated as law. It is written
as a rule of decision, not a preamble, so that it can be cited in a motion.

*Drafting notes on (D) — why both, and why these numbers.* The brief asks whether 30, 60 or
90 days, or a count of denials, or a percentage, produces the cleanest rule, and directs
that numbers not be adopted without research. The research produces a clear answer on
*form* and a judgment call on *number*:

- **A count is more administrable than a percentage.** Texas, Illinois and Minnesota all
  index escalation to counts (three contempt findings; two prior convictions; "repeatedly").
  No researched jurisdiction uses a percentage-of-ordered-time threshold for this purpose.
  A percentage requires computing a denominator, which varies by schedule, holiday
  rotation, and season — bad for a police officer or a prosecutor.
- **A percentage catches what a count misses.** A parent who takes the child out of state
  for five weeks commits one continuous denial, not three. Division (D)(2) exists for that
  case.
- **Three in 90 days** is chosen because three is the count every researched jurisdiction
  converges on, and 90 days is long enough to exclude a bad fortnight and short enough to
  establish a course of conduct. A 30-day window would let a parent deny every other weekend
  indefinitely without ever crossing the line; a 12-month window would make the trigger
  nearly automatic in any high-conflict case.
- **50 per cent in 30 days** is retained from the brief's working proposal, unchanged,
  because nothing in the research contradicts it and it is the natural measure for a single
  extended deprivation.
- **The most important design choice is what (D) does *not* do.** It does not make the
  trigger an element of the crime by itself, and it does not presume intent. It defines a
  term. The criminal offence in § 2919.232 requires the State to prove the knowing course of
  conduct independently.
- **Texas's alternative is better still where it is available, and § 3109.0881(D) adopts
  it:** index escalation to *prior contempt findings* rather than to alleged incidents. That
  is self-proving from the docket and cannot be inflated.

### § 3109.0881 — Expedited enforcement of parenting time
- **(A) Motion.** Contents as in S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0491(B) — order date,
  description of the time awarded, dates and amount of time lost, efforts made to exercise
  it, and relief requested. Adopted unchanged.
- **(B) Hearing.** Not later than 28 days after service, unless good cause is shown.
  *(S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0491(C), unchanged.)*
- **(C) Proof.** The order plus evidence of the missed period establishes a prima facie
  case. The burden then shifts to the responding parent to show lawful justification.
- **(D) The ladder.**

| Stage | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **First proven unjustified denial** | Make-up parenting time of the same type and duration, within six months — **mandatory**. Written warning. |
| **Second and subsequent** | Make-up time, **plus** the court shall order at least one of: reasonable costs and attorney fees; a civil sanction up to $500 per denied period; parent education at the violating parent's expense; supervised or neutral-location exchanges; or a bond conditioned on compliance. |
| **Three or more findings** | Make-up time in **twice** the duration denied; costs and fees **may not be waived**; and the pattern **constitutes a change in circumstances** for purposes of R.C. 3109.04(E)(1)(a) (Track B) or S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0419 (Track A). |

  *(Tex. Fam. Code §§ 156.107, 157.167(e), 157.168(a), (a-2), (d), all eff. Sept. 1, 2025;
  Minn. Stat. § 518.175, subd. 6; 750 ILCS 5/607.5; A.R.S. § 25-414.)*
- **(E) De minimis.** No sanction beyond make-up time for a denial that was inadvertent, of
  minimal duration, or promptly cured, and a court may decline to find a violation where the
  parties agreed to the change.
- **(F)** Existing remedies preserved: R.C. 3109.051(K), R.C. 2705.031, and S.B. 174
  proposed R.C. 3109.0493 all remain available and cumulative.

*Drafting notes.* (C) is the "mechanically understandable" enforcement the brief asks for
and is the most important division in the Part: it converts the proceeding from a
credibility contest into a documents case. (E) is the counterweight the brief also asks for
— harmless scheduling errors must not become litigation — and it is drafted before the
criminal section so that the same de minimis principle is visible in both.

### § 3109.0882 — Good-faith reports protected
- **(A)** A report of suspected child abuse or neglect, or of criminal conduct, made in good
  faith is not interference under § 3109.088 and not an offence under § 2919.232, **and may
  not be considered against the reporting parent under any best-interest factor.**
- **(B)** A report is not a violation merely because it was unsubstantiated, was not
  prosecuted, was not proven, or was mistaken.
- **(C)** Only a statement that was **materially false as to fact**, that the person **knew
  was false**, and that was made **for the purpose** of preventing lawful parenting time or
  impairing the parent-child relationship may qualify.
- **(D)** R.C. 2151.421, including its reporting duties and its immunity, is unaffected.
- **(E)** A parent who declines to permit parenting time while a report is pending has
  lawful justification if the parent reported promptly and in good faith and complies with
  the agency's or court's direction.

*Drafting notes.* (C) is the KRS 403.270(4)(h) triple guard. (E) is the practical provision
without which (A) is hollow: the parent who reports in good faith and withholds the child
for a weekend while the agency responds must not be exposed. Note that (A)'s final clause
goes further than existing Ohio law and further than S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0430(10),
which makes a *knowingly false* report a factor; (A) makes clear the converse is also
true — a good-faith report is not a demerit.

### § 3109.089 — Child's refusal; duty to facilitate
- **(A)** A parent shall make reasonable efforts to ensure the child is available for and
  complies with court-ordered parenting time.
- **(B)** A child's refusal is not lawful justification unless the parent made reasonable
  efforts and promptly notified the other parent.
- **(C)** Reasonable efforts do not require physical force.
- **(D)** The court may consider the wishes of a child of sufficient age and maturity, under
  R.C. 3109.04(B) (Track B) or S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0430(A)(2) and 3109.0445–3109.0451
  (Track A). Nothing in this section limits that consideration.
- **(E)** A repeated pattern of a child's refusal is a ground for the court to inquire, on
  its own motion, into the cause.

*Drafting notes.* (A)–(C) give "the child doesn't want to go" a legal answer without
requiring a parent to do something impossible or harmful. (C) is essential; without it the
duty in (A) is unreasonable and the provision would be unenforceable in practice and
indefensible in testimony. (E) is the provision that keeps (A)–(C) honest in the other
direction: a child's persistent refusal may be evidence of something the court needs to know
about either parent.

### § 2919.232 — Interference with parenting time [NEW CRIMINAL OFFENCE]
**Number chosen because R.C. 2919.231 is occupied** ("Interfering with action to issue or
modify support order"), whose structure this section follows.

- **(A) Offence.** "No person having custody or control of a child shall knowingly and
  without lawful justification engage in a course of conduct that substantially interferes
  with another parent's exercise of parenting time under a court order or with the other
  parent's legally established relationship with the child."
- **(B) Course of conduct.** Requires three or more knowing, unjustified denials within any
  90-day period, **or** a single knowing, unjustified deprivation of 30 or more consecutive
  days.
- **(C) Lawful justification — the State's burden.** The State must prove the absence of
  lawful justification. Lawful justification exists where the person acted in compliance with
  another valid court order; reasonably believed the action necessary to prevent imminent
  physical harm to the child or to the person; responded in good faith to suspected child
  abuse or sexual abuse in accordance with § 3109.0882; acted because of a medical
  emergency; acted because of circumstances beyond the person's control, including illness,
  weather, or transportation failure; or acted with the other parent's agreement.
  *(Minn. Stat. § 609.26, subd. 2, adapted; R.C. 2919.23(C).)*
- **(D) Express exclusions.** No violation for an accident, a misunderstanding, an agreed
  schedule change, illness, a weather emergency, a circumstance outside the person's control,
  or a reasonable good-faith protective action.
- **(E) Non-exclusivity.** "The availability of contempt, modification, or another civil
  remedy does not preclude investigation or prosecution under this section." *(Codifies
  Toledo v. Parra, 2013-Ohio-3182, ¶¶ 17–18.)*
- **(F) Penalties.**
  - First offence: **misdemeanor of the first degree.**
  - **Felony of the fifth degree** if: the offender has a prior conviction under this
    section; the offender committed the conduct after a court found intentional interference
    under § 3109.088; the child was concealed; the child was removed from Ohio to defeat the
    other parent's parenting time; the conduct involved a threat of or actual violence; or
    the deprivation exceeded 90 consecutive days.
  *(Tracks R.C. 2919.23(D)(2) and R.C. 2919.231(B).)*
- **(G)** Nothing in the section applies to conduct authorised or required by a protection
  order or by a public children services agency.

*Drafting notes on the amend-or-enact question.* The brief asks whether to amend
R.C. 2919.23 instead. **Recommendation: enact R.C. 2919.232 and leave R.C. 2919.23 alone.**
Three reasons. (1) R.C. 2919.23's elements — "entice, take, keep, or harbor … *from* the
parent" — describe child-stealing. *Parra* held they *can* reach a custodial parent's refusal
to hand the child over, but only by reading "keep … from" to cover standing still, which is
why prosecutors decline these cases. Purpose-built elements fix the actual problem.
(2) R.C. 2919.23 carries thirty years of case law across multiple districts (*Sprinkle*,
*Brickles*, *Skelly*, *Jenkins*); amending its core verbs would unsettle all of it for no
gain. (3) A separate section can carry its own mens rea, its own safe harbours, its own
course-of-conduct requirement and its own de minimis exclusions without those provisions
spilling onto genuine abduction cases — where, for instance, a "circumstances beyond the
person's control" defence would be inappropriate. *Parra*'s holding that prosecution is
available survives untouched and is now codified in (E).

*Drafting notes on why (B) is strict.* Three denials in 90 days, or 30 continuous days, is
a materially higher threshold than the civil trigger in § 3109.088(D). That gap is
deliberate: the civil system should engage on the first denial and the criminal system
should not. Compare 720 ILCS 5/10-5.5, where the first two offences are petty offences and
only the third is a Class A misdemeanor — Illinois reaches a similar place by grading rather
than by threshold. Either design works; this one keeps first-time and second-time
parenting-time disputes out of criminal court entirely, which is the more conservative and
more defensible choice.

## PART 6 — RELOCATION, RECORDS, TRANSITION, SUPPORT, AND DATA

### § 3109.0891 — Relocation
Three divisions, and nothing more, because S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0470–3109.0479
already provide a complete relocation regime and current R.C. 3109.051(G) provides the
existing one.

- **(A)** A parent shall not relocate primarily for the purpose of defeating the other
  parent's parenting time or the child's relationship with the other parent, and the court
  shall consider whether a relocation was undertaken for that purpose.
- **(B)** Where distance makes equal parenting time impracticable, the court shall maximize
  parenting time under § 3109.0863 through extended blocks, school breaks, summers,
  holidays, and reasonable electronic contact, and shall allocate transportation
  responsibility and cost.
- **(C)** Nothing requires equal parenting time where geography makes it impracticable.

*Drafting notes.* (C) is stated expressly because the distance case was the Ohio State Bar
Association's most persuasive illustration against H.B. 14 and because saying it out loud
costs the policy nothing.

### § 3109.0892 — Records and information access
**One division only.** Both legal parents have equal direct access to school records,
medical records, extracurricular information, schedules, and emergency information unless a
court order specifically restricts it, and a school, health care provider, or child care
provider shall not treat one legal parent as subordinate because the parents live separately
or were never married.

*Drafting notes.* This is deliberately the shortest section in the Act. Current
R.C. 3109.051(H)–(J), R.C. 3319.321, R.C. 5104.039 and R.C. 3109.11 already cover most of
this ground, and S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0516–3109.0529 recodify and **expand** it by
removing the residential/non-residential distinction entirely. Duplicating that work would
violate the brief's rule 12. The only genuine gap is the unmarried-parent case, where the
existing sections operate off a residential-parent designation that § 3109.085 eliminates,
and the single sentence above closes it.

### Section 3 (uncodified) — Transition
- **(A)** The Act applies to proceedings commenced on or after the effective date and to
  initial determinations made on or after that date.
- **(B)** An order entered before the effective date remains valid and enforceable.
- **(C)** **Enactment of the Act is not, by itself, a change in circumstances** under
  R.C. 3109.04(E)(1)(a) or S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0419, and is not by itself grounds to
  modify a prior order.
- **(D)** The enforcement provisions of § 3109.0881, the definition in § 3109.088, and the
  offence in § 2919.232 **do** apply to conduct occurring on or after the effective date,
  whatever the date of the order violated.
- **(E)** Nothing limits the modification pathway in S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.0498(A).

*Drafting notes.* (C) is the direct answer to the floodgate objection and is stated in the
same terms the objection used. (D) is the necessary carve-out: an order entered in 2019 is
still an order, and a parent who defies it in 2028 should face the new enforcement regime.
Kentucky, West Virginia and Arkansas all applied their presumptions to new determinations
without an express retroactivity rule; Florida's silence on the point generated appellate
litigation. Saying it is free.

### Section 4 (uncodified) — Child support conforming provisions
- **(A)** Equal parenting time does not by itself eliminate a child support obligation;
  incomes may differ substantially.
- **(B)** R.C. 3119.051 (the 10 per cent adjustment at 90 or more overnights) and
  R.C. 3119.231 (deviation consideration above 90 overnights) apply as written to orders
  entered under the Act, and S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3119.071 and 3119.072 apply if enacted.
- **(C)** No change is made to the basic child support schedule, the worksheet, or any
  deviation standard.

*Drafting notes.* This section exists to be able to say, truthfully, that the bill does not
touch child support. The Ohio State Bar Association's H.B. 14 testimony flagged the
overnight-linked support reduction as a financial incentive to litigate. That incentive is
created by R.C. 3119.051, not by this Act, and this Act leaves it exactly as it found it.
Division (A) is the one substantive line, and it works against the incentive rather than
with it.

### Section 5 (uncodified) — Data and review
The Supreme Court of Ohio is requested to compile and publish annually, from data reported
by the courts of common pleas: the number of initial allocations; the distribution of
parenting-time arrangements by approximate percentage; the number of cases in which the
presumption was rebutted and the grounds; the number of departures below the minimum
schedule; and the number of enforcement motions filed and their disposition.

*Drafting notes.* Revived from H.B. 508. Requested of the Supreme Court rather than mandated
of the courts of common pleas, to match the drafting posture of S.B. 174 Section 4. This is
the only provision in the Act that National Parents Organization (June 3, 2026: "Include in
the legislation a requirement to generate and evaluate metrics for success") and the
institutional bar have separately asked for.

### Section 6 (uncodified) — Contingency
- **(A)** If S.B. 174 of the 136th General Assembly (or substantially similar legislation) is
  enacted and takes effect, the cross-references in this Act operate as the Track A
  references, "designated parent and legal custodian" and "parenting plan" carry their
  S.B. 174 meanings, § 3109.086 through § 3109.0863 apply to the approval or issuance of a
  parenting plan under S.B. 174 proposed R.C. 3109.046 to 3109.048, and S.B. 174 proposed
  R.C. 3109.0424 and 3109.0412 are repealed.
- **(B)** Otherwise the Track B references operate, and R.C. 3109.042 is repealed.
- **(C)** The Legislative Service Commission is authorised to make conforming
  cross-reference corrections.

## THE JUDGE'S DECISION TREE

The Act is designed so that a judge in a routine case answers three questions.

```
Q1  Has a party proved by a preponderance that equal parenting time
    is not in the child's best interest?                    § 3109.086

    NO  → order approximately equal parenting time. Done. No findings required.

    YES → state the two findings (§ 3109.0861) and go to Q2.

Q2  What schedule maximizes each parent's time consistent with the
    child's welfare?                                        § 3109.0863

    → order that schedule, but not less than the minimum, and go to Q3
      only if the answer would be less than the minimum.

Q3  Is there a preponderance showing that a restriction is reasonably
    calculated to protect the child from abuse or a parent from
    domestic violence?                          § 3109.087(C), § 3109.0871

    NO  → the minimum schedule applies.

    YES → state the safety finding and impose the least restrictive
          arrangement reasonably necessary.
```

And in an enforcement case, three more:

```
E1  Was parenting time ordered?                      § 3109.0881(C)
E2  Was it denied?                                   § 3109.0881(C)
E3  Was the denial intentional and without lawful justification?
                                                     § 3109.0881(C), (E)

    E1 + E2 proved → prima facie case; burden shifts.
    E3 unrebutted  → make-up time is mandatory; then apply the ladder
                     in § 3109.0881(D).
```

No multi-factor balancing is required at any node. Every node is either a documents
question or a single finding.
