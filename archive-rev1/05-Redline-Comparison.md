## 1. Basic architecture of allocation

| Current law | S.B. 174 (As Passed by Senate) | This proposal |
|---|---|---|
| R.C. 3109.04(A): two routes only. Either (1) allocate "primarily to one of the parents," designate that parent "the residential parent and the legal custodian," and divide the rest; or (2) if a parent filed a pleading/motion under (G) **and** a shared parenting plan **and** the plan is in the child's best interest, issue a shared parenting order. | Proposed R.C. 3109.041(A): "A court shall allocate parenting responsibilities in any proceeding pertaining to the allocation of parenting responsibilities." Proposed R.C. 3109.042: allocation "shall be based on a parenting plan approved or issued by the court." **Repeals shared parenting and sole custody as concepts.** Proposed R.C. 3109.048: if no one files a plan, the court issues one. | Adopts S.B. 174's architecture unchanged (Track A). Under Track B, adds R.C. 3109.04(A)(3): if the presumptions are not rebutted the court allocates to both parents, orders equal parenting time and joint major decisions, **and no pleading, motion, or plan under division (G) is required.** |
| **Effect:** shared parenting is opt-in and pleading-dependent. A parent who files nothing gets sole-custody allocation by default. | **Effect:** the plan/no-plan dichotomy disappears; every case produces a plan. This is a real improvement independent of any presumption. | **Effect:** the pleading barrier is removed in both tracks, so the presumption has something to operate on. |

## 2. Is there a presumption? — the central difference

| Current law | S.B. 174 (As Passed by Senate) | This proposal |
|---|---|---|
| **None.** No presumption for or against any schedule. R.C. 3109.051(A): "Whenever possible, the order or decree … shall ensure the opportunity for both parents to have frequent and continuing contact with the child." Aspirational, not a burden-allocating rule. | **None.** Proposed R.C. 3109.044: the plan "shall seek to … maximize parenting time with each parent **when it is in the best interest of the child**." Proposed R.C. 3109.046 and 3109.047: where a plan "includes a provision for substantially equal parenting time, the court **may deny the request**" if it is "not in the best interest of the child, endangers the safety of the parties, **or for other good cause shown**," with written findings. Proposed R.C. 3109.0412: "The court shall have **complete discretion** over the approval of a parenting plan." *House substitute: flips R.C. 3109.044 to "unless it is not in the best interest"; adds "special weight" to joint plans; **deletes R.C. 3109.0412**.* | **§ 3109.086(A):** "There is a presumption, rebuttable by a preponderance of the evidence, that equal parenting time is in the best interest of the child." **§ 3109.086(B):** parallel presumption for major decisions, rebutted independently. |
| | **Assessment.** S.B. 174 requires a *reason*, not a *starting point*. "Other good cause shown" is an open-ended third ground, and proposed R.C. 3109.0412 restores in one sentence whatever the findings requirement constrains. This is the gap the proposal fills. | Repeals proposed R.C. 3109.0412 under Track A (already deleted by the House substitute). Does **not** disturb the "other good cause" language in proposed R.C. 3109.046/3109.047 — instead, § 3109.086 supplies the burden those sections lack, and the contingency section makes plan approval subject to § 3109.086. |

## 7. Parents who were never married to each other — the untouched gap

|---|---|---|---|

## 11. False reports

## 20. Net summary — what the proposal adds, keeps, and removes

**Added to S.B. 174's framework (11 things):**
1. A rebuttable presumption of equal parenting time (§ 3109.086(A)).
2. A parallel, independently rebuttable presumption of shared major decision-making (§ 3109.086(B)).
3. Equal parental status on establishment of parentage, with no separate action required (§ 3109.085).
4. A statewide minimum parenting time schedule, expressly a floor and not a target (§ 3109.087).
5. A mandatory maximize-time fallback (§ 3109.0863).
6. Front-loaded domestic-violence findings and the anti-weaponization clause (§ 3109.0862).
7. A reverse presumption on two or more found acts of domestic violence (§ 3109.086(G)).
8. Burden-shifting prima facie proof plus a three-rung enforcement ladder (§ 3109.0881).
9. An objective, conduct-based definition of interference with express anti-syndrome language (§ 3109.088).
10. A narrow criminal offence codifying *Parra*'s non-exclusivity holding (R.C. 2919.232).
11. An annual data report (Section 5).

**Kept from S.B. 174 unchanged:** the parenting-plan architecture; all terminology; the
26 best-interest factors; the preponderance standard for restrictions and the restriction
grounds and menu (proposed R.C. 3109.0455–3109.0459); the 28-day hearing clocks
(proposed R.C. 3109.0423, 3109.0491(C)); the relocation regime
(proposed R.C. 3109.0470–3109.0479); records and access (proposed R.C. 3109.0516–3109.0529);
appellate priority (proposed R.C. 3109.0428); GAL and child-interview provisions; child support
(proposed R.C. 3119.071, 3119.072).

**Removed from S.B. 174 (2 things, one already gone):**
1. Proposed R.C. 3109.0412, "complete discretion over the approval of a parenting plan" —
   *already deleted by the pending House substitute*.
2. Proposed R.C. 3109.0424, the unmarried-mother sole-custodian rule.

**Rejected from H.B. 14 and H.B. 508 (5 things):** the clear-and-convincing standard; the
"detrimental to the child" test; the rebuttal-factor lists in proposed R.C. 3109.0421 and
3109.0422 (superseded by § 3109.086(E) and S.B. 174's own factor list); the post-rebuttal
channeling of majority time to the "more likely to facilitate" parent in proposed
R.C. 3109.0424–3109.0425 (superseded by the maximize-time fallback, which does not require
the court to pick a winner); and silence on transition.
