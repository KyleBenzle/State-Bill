## TITLE

To amend sections 2919.23, 3109.03, 3109.04, 3109.042, 3109.043, 3109.051, 3109.12, and
3109.401 and to enact sections 3109.044, 3109.045, and 3109.046 of the Revised Code to give
every legal parent equal parental status whether or not the parents were married; to establish
a presumption of shared parenting with substantially equal parenting time; to let a parent under
an existing order request equal parental status; to provide that only a court may change a
parenting order; to require that an allegation restricting a parent's time be resolved; and to
strengthen enforcement of parenting time orders.

# PART TWO — THE STARTING POINT

## Sec. 3109.044 — enacted. Presumption of shared parenting and substantially equal time.

**(A) Presumption.** When a court first allocates parental rights and responsibilities for the
care of a child, including by a temporary order, there is a presumption, rebuttable by a
preponderance of the evidence, that shared parenting with substantially equal parenting time is
in the best interest of the child.

**(B) Order if not rebutted.** If the presumption is not rebutted, the court shall issue a shared
parenting decree under which both parents are residential parents and legal custodians, major
decisions are made jointly, and parenting time is substantially equal. Neither parent is
required to file a pleading, motion, or plan under division (G) of section 3109.04 of the Revised
Code. If no plan is filed, the court shall issue one.

**(C) When the presumption does not apply.** The presumption does not apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the parents agree on the allocation and the court approves the agreement;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** As to a parent against whom a protection order or consent agreement under section
2151.34, 2903.213, 2903.214, 2919.26, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code protects the other parent or
the child;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** As to a parent described in the second paragraph of division (C) of section 3109.04
of the Revised Code (conviction for, or adjudication as perpetrator of, child abuse, child
neglect, domestic violence, or a sexually oriented offense against a family or household member).

**(D) What does not rebut the presumption.** None of the following, by itself, rebuts the
presumption: conflict between the parents; one parent's objection; a lack of cooperation caused
by the parent relying on it; or an allegation the court has not found to be true.

**(E) What a court must do if the presumption is rebutted.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The court shall state in writing the facts that rebut the presumption.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Unless the parents agree, the court shall order a parenting time schedule that
maximizes each parent's time with the child, consistent with the child's welfare.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The court shall not give a parent fewer than ninety overnights a year, or the
equivalent in parenting time, unless the parents agree, that parent asks for less, distance
makes it impracticable, or the court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that more time
would endanger the child's physical or emotional welfare. If the court orders less, it shall
order the least restrictive arrangement that protects the child and state its reasons in
writing. **Ninety overnights is a floor. It is not a standard, target, or preferred schedule.**

**(F) Decision-making and time are separate.** The court may find the presumption rebutted as to
parenting time but not as to joint decision-making, or the reverse.

**(G) Joint decisions.** Under joint decision-making, each parent makes day-to-day and emergency
decisions while the child is with that parent. If the parents cannot agree on a major decision,
either may ask the court to decide it, and the existing arrangement continues until the court
does.

**(H) Safety.** A court may restrict a parent's rights or parenting time to protect the child or
a parent from physical harm, abuse, or domestic violence on a finding by a preponderance of the
evidence. A criminal charge or conviction is not required. Nothing in this section limits
Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code or any protection order.

**(I) Definition.** "Substantially equal parenting time" means each parent has at least
forty-five per cent of overnights, or the equivalent in parenting time, measured over a year.

# PART THREE — ONLY A COURT CHANGES A COURT ORDER

## Sec. 3109.045 — enacted. Court orders control.

**(A)** An order allocating parental rights and responsibilities or providing for parenting time
stays in effect as written until a court changes it. No one else may change, suspend, or add
conditions to the order. That includes a parent, guardian ad litem, parenting coordinator,
mediator, evaluator, counselor, therapist, law enforcement officer, and any employee of a public
children services agency or child support enforcement agency. A recommendation, report,
allegation, safety plan, or instruction from any of them does not change the order. Parents may
agree to change a particular exchange or period of parenting time. That agreement does not change
the order.

**(B)** A guardian ad litem may investigate, gather information, observe, report, testify, and
make recommendations as provided by law and the Rules of Superintendence. A recommendation is
evidence for the court to weigh. It has no legal effect unless the court adopts it in an order.
A party may cross-examine the guardian ad litem about the report and the basis for any
recommendation. A
court shall not give a guardian ad litem or any other person who is not a judge or magistrate
authority to change, suspend, supervise, or condition parenting time or the allocation of
parental rights and responsibilities. A provision of an order that purports to give that
authority is unenforceable.

**(C)** A parenting coordinator may help parents carry out an order and may decide disputes over
how it is carried out, within the authority given by the appointing order and the Rules of
Superintendence. A parenting coordinator may not reduce or suspend a parent's parenting time,
require supervision, or change the allocation of parental rights and responsibilities. A
parenting coordinator's decision is subject to independent review by the court on a parent's
objection.

**(D)** A public children services agency safety plan restricts a parent's court-ordered
parenting time only if that parent agrees in writing or a court orders the restriction. An agency
that believes a child is in danger may seek an emergency order under Chapter 2151. of the Revised
Code.

**(E)** This section does not limit any of the following: a protection order; taking a child into
custody under section 2151.31 of the Revised Code; an emergency or ex parte order of a court; or
action reasonably necessary to prevent imminent physical harm to a person.

# PART FOUR — ALLEGATIONS MUST BE RESOLVED

## Sec. 3109.046 — enacted. Allegations; outside limit; false allegations.

**(A) Definitions.** In this section:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** "Restriction" means a court order, or part of an order, that suspends, requires
supervision of, or materially reduces a parent's parenting time or parental rights and
responsibilities, because of an allegation of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or other
misconduct by that parent.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** An allegation is "resolved" when a court, after a hearing at which the restricted
parent could present evidence, has found whether the allegation is true. That finding may be
made in the proceeding where the restriction was issued, in an adjudication under Chapter 2151.
of the Revised Code, in a full hearing on a protection order, or by a criminal conviction. None
of the following, by itself, resolves an allegation: a children services agency's
determination; a decision to charge or not to charge; dismissal of a charge; or an acquittal.

**(B) An allegation does not restrict anyone by itself.** Making an allegation, a report, or a
filing does not by itself change a parenting order. Only a court order can impose a restriction,
and the restriction must rest on evidence.

**(C) Prompt hearing.** On the restricted parent's request, the court shall hold an evidentiary
hearing on the allegation within ninety days after the request unless good cause is shown. A
pending investigation, or the absence of a criminal charge, does not by itself prevent the court
from deciding whether the allegation is true.

**(D) Thirty-six-month outside limit.** A restriction based on an unresolved allegation ends
thirty-six months after it first took effect. If the allegation is still unresolved thirty months
after the restriction took effect, the court shall set it for hearing on its own motion. Before
the thirty-six months end, the court may grant one
continuance of not more than ninety days if it finds in writing that additional time is genuinely
necessary to resolve the allegation and states why. No further continuance may be granted. When
the restriction ends, the parenting order that applied before it took effect resumes. If there
was none, the court shall issue a parenting time order under section 3109.044 of the Revised Code
within twenty-eight days, without relying on the unresolved allegation. The same or a
substantially similar allegation cannot support a new restriction unless it is resolved. A
restriction may be based on new conduct.

**(E) After resolution.** If the court finds the allegation true, it may continue, change, or end
the restriction in the child's best interest. If the court finds it not true, the restriction
ends.

**(F) Good-faith reports protected.** An allegation or report made in good faith shall not be held
against the person who made it. An allegation is not false merely because it was
unsubstantiated, not prosecuted, not proven, or mistaken. A parent's good-faith action to protect
the child or the parent from abuse or domestic violence shall not be counted against that parent
under division (F)(1)(f) of section 3109.04 or division (D)(10) of section 3109.051 of the
Revised Code.

**(G) Knowingly false allegations.** If the court finds by a preponderance of the evidence that a
parent made an allegation that was materially false, that the parent knew was false, and that
the parent made in order to interfere with the other parent's relationship or parenting time with
the child, all of the following apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The finding is a change in circumstances for purposes of division (E)(1)(a) of
section 3109.04 of the Revised Code and a factor in the child's best interest.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** The court shall order compensatory parenting time for time lost because of the
allegation, under division (K) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** The court shall order the parent to pay the other parent's reasonable attorney's
fees and costs caused by the allegation.

**(H) Scope.** This section does not apply to a protection order or to a case under Chapter 2151.
of the Revised Code, which have their own procedures. It applies to a restriction in a case under
this chapter that relies on either of them. Nothing in this section limits section 2151.421 of the
Revised Code, including its reporting duties and its immunity for good-faith reports.

# PART SEVEN — UNCODIFIED SECTIONS

**Section 3. Existing orders.**

(A) Sections 3109.044 and 3109.043(B) and (C) of the Revised Code apply to any allocation decided
on or after the effective date of this act, including in a case pending on that date.

(B) An order in effect on the effective date stays in effect. A parent may seek equal status under
division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code. Otherwise, enactment of this act is not
a change in circumstances and is not by itself grounds to modify an order.

(C) For a restriction in effect on the effective date, the period in division (D) of section
3109.046 of the Revised Code ends on the later of thirty-six months after the restriction first
took effect or twelve months after the effective date.

(D) Divisions (K)(2) to (6) of section 3109.051 and divisions (E) and (G) of section 2919.23 of the
Revised Code apply to conduct on or after the effective date, whatever the date of the order.

**Section 4. Child support.** This act does not change how child support is calculated. An order
under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code is not by itself grounds to modify
child support.

**Section 5. Data.** The General Assembly requests that the Supreme Court of Ohio publish each year,
from data reported by courts: the number of initial allocations and how many ordered substantially
equal parenting time; the number of motions under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the
Revised Code and how many were granted; and the number of referrals under division (K)(6) of
section 3109.051 of the Revised Code and how many were declined.

**Section 6. S.B. 174.** If Sub. S.B. 174 of the 136th General Assembly is enacted, the General
Assembly intends that this act be conformed to the corresponding sections of that act. *(A
conforming amendment would be needed. See Document 2, Part 3.)*
