### The bill in eight lines

1. **Equal status.** Once parentage is legally established, both parents are equal residential
   parents and legal custodians, married or not. No lawsuit is needed. *Status is not a
   schedule.*
2. **50/50 start.** Every first allocation, including temporary orders, starts from shared
   parenting with substantially equal time, rebuttable by a preponderance of the evidence. If the
   start is rebutted, the court must maximize each parent's time, with a 90-overnight floor that is
   not a target.
3. **Existing orders.** A parent may move for equal status with no change in circumstances. The
   court grants it absent proven violence or abuse or an immediate threat. **The schedule does not
   change** until a court changes it.
4. **Only a court changes a court order.** GALs, parenting coordinators, caseworkers, and police
   cannot change one. A GAL recommendation is evidence, not an order.
5. **Allegations must be resolved.** The restricted parent can get a hearing within 90 days. A
   36-month outside limit applies, with a hearing set at 30 months and one continuance of up to 90
   days. Good-faith reports are protected; knowingly false ones have consequences.
6. **Enforcement.** Make-up time becomes mandatory, with a 28-day hearing and a three-part prima
   facie case. A third contempt finding is a change in circumstances.
7. **Criminal.** Knowing, repeated withholding is an offense under the existing R.C. 2919.23. It is
   "not a civil matter."
8. **Referral.** A court may refer conduct to a prosecutor, who must charge or send a written
   declination within 60 days (90 with one extension). No reasons are required.

### Read first

- **Drafted against current law, not S.B. 174.** S.B. 174 is still in House Judiciary, which
  accepted a substitute on 13 May 2026 but has not reported the bill. It dies on 31 December 2026
  unless enacted. See Document 2, Part 3.
- **One deliberate narrowing of existing law.** Plain withholding of parenting time becomes a crime
  only when it is knowing and repeated. *Toledo v. Parra* would reach a single reckless instance.
  See Document 2, Part 8, and Option 3.
- **Missing planning notes.** The enforcement-referral body and the 36-month mechanism come from
  planning discussions that are not in these files. They were rebuilt from the written instructions.
  The referral body defaults to the court, with a Michigan Friend-of-the-Court-style agency as
  Option 4. Check both against those discussions.
