# DOCUMENT 3 — SECTION-BY-SECTION OUTLINE (REVISION 2)

The bill has **11 codified sections**: 8 existing sections amended and 3 new sections enacted. It
also has 4 uncodified sections. The bill is grouped below by what each part does for a parent.

| Part | What it does | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| One | Who is a parent | 3109.03, 3109.042 (amended) |
| Two | The 50/50 starting point, and equal status under old orders | **3109.044 (new)**; 3109.04, 3109.043, 3109.12, 3109.401 (amended) |
| Three | Only a court changes an order | **3109.045 (new)** |
| Four | Allegations must be resolved | **3109.046 (new)** |
| Five | Enforcement | 3109.051(K) (amended) |
| Six | Criminal interference | 2919.23 (amended) |
| Seven | Transition, child support, data, S.B. 174 | Uncodified Sections 3–6 |

## PART ONE — WHO IS A PARENT

### R.C. 3109.03 (amended): equality of parents
- **Today:** "husband and wife" stand upon an equality.
- **Change:** "the parents of a child," including parents who were never married. A court may not
  prefer a parent by sex or marital status.
- **Why here:** this is Ohio's existing equality sentence. Extending it is one phrase.

### R.C. 3109.042 (amended): parents who were never married
- **(A)** Once the second parent's parentage is final (by an acknowledgment of paternity or a
  Chapter 3111 determination), **both parents are residential parents and legal custodians.** No
  lawsuit is needed. Before then, the mother is the sole custodian, as the only legal parent.
- **(B)** This is status only. No schedule is created. Either parent may seek an order.
- **(C)** An existing custody or parenting-time order keeps governing. The parent's route is the
  motion under 3109.04(E)(1)(c).
- **(D)** The rape and sexual battery bar is kept and made sex-neutral.
- **Replaces:** the "unmarried female … is the sole residential parent" rule.

## PART TWO — THE STARTING POINT

### R.C. 3109.044 (new): presumption of shared parenting and substantially equal time
- **(A)** At every first allocation, including temporary orders, shared parenting with
  substantially equal time is presumed to be in the child's best interest. It is rebuttable by a
  preponderance of the evidence.
- **(B)** If not rebutted, the court issues a shared parenting decree. No party needs to file a
  plan.
- **(C)** It does not apply where the parents agree, as to a protection-order respondent, or as to a
  3109.04(C) offender.
- **(D)** Not enough on their own: conflict, objection, self-created non-cooperation, or an
  allegation the court has not found true.
- **(E)** If rebutted:
  - written facts;
  - a maximize-time schedule;
  - a floor of **90 overnights**, with exceptions for agreement, the parent's own request, distance,
    or endangerment;
  - least restrictive means;
  - "floor, not target."
- **(F)** Time and decision-making are rebutted separately.
- **(G)** Joint decisions: day-to-day and emergency decisions belong to the parent who has the
  child; on deadlock, the status quo continues until the court rules.
- **(H)** Safety: a court may restrict on a preponderance. No conviction is needed. Chapter 2151 and
  protection orders are preserved.
- **(I)** "Substantially equal" means 45 percent or more of overnights, or the equivalent.

### R.C. 3109.04 (amended): allocation
- **(A):** allocation is made under 3109.044, which controls over the plan-filing requirements.
- **(E)(1)(c) (new): equal status under an existing order.**
  - Motion. No change in circumstances is required, and no plan.
  - The court **shall grant** unless one of these is found:
    - a proven history of physical violence or abuse;
    - an immediate threat of physical harm;
    - an existing statutory bar.
  - The result is a shared parenting decree.
  - **The schedule, support, school, and all other terms carry over unchanged.** There is no
    self-help.
  - It may be joined with a parenting-time motion, which is decided on best interest (*Braatz*).
  - After a denial, there is a 12-month wait to refile.
- **(E)(1)(a):** enactment is not by itself a change in circumstances.

### R.C. 3109.043 (amended): temporary orders
- **(B)** An oral hearing within 28 days on request. The presumption applies there.
- **(C)** No inference from a temporary order. Its length, and the child's adjustment to it, are not
  by themselves reasons to keep it.

### R.C. 3109.12 (amended)
- The presumption applies to parenting-time orders under this section.

### R.C. 3109.401 (amended)
- The "no standard schedule" policy sentence is made "subject to section 3109.044."

## PART THREE — ONLY A COURT CHANGES A COURT ORDER

### R.C. 3109.045 (new): court orders control
- **(A)** An order stays in effect as written until a court changes it. GALs, parenting
  coordinators, counselors, caseworkers, officers, and parents cannot change it. Parents may agree to
  swap a particular exchange or period.
- **(B)** A GAL investigates, reports, testifies, and recommends. A recommendation is evidence with
  no effect until adopted in an order. A party may cross-examine the GAL. Any delegation to a
  non-judge of power over parenting time is unenforceable.
- **(C)** A parenting coordinator decides implementation disputes within its Sup.R. 6.40–6.46
  authority. It may not reduce or suspend time, impose supervision, or change custody. On a parent's
  objection, the court reviews the decision independently.
- **(D)** A children-services safety plan binds court-ordered time only with the parent's written
  agreement or a court order.
- **(E)** Protection orders, emergency custody, ex parte orders, and action to prevent imminent harm
  are preserved.

## PART FOUR — ALLEGATIONS MUST BE RESOLVED

### R.C. 3109.046 (new): allegations; outside limit; false allegations
- **(A)** Definitions:
  - **"Restriction":** an allegation-based reduction of a parent's time or rights.
  - **"Resolved":** a court finding, after a hearing, on whether the allegation is true. Agency
    dispositions, charging decisions, dismissals, and acquittals are not resolutions.
- **(B)** An allegation is not self-executing. Only a court order, based on evidence, restricts a
  parent.
- **(C)** A hearing within 90 days on the restricted parent's request. A pending investigation, or no
  charge, does not stop the court from deciding.
- **(D)** The outside limit:
  - a 36-month cap;
  - the court self-sets a hearing at 30 months;
  - one continuance of up to 90 days, on written findings;
  - the prior order then resumes;
  - no recycling of the same allegation; new conduct may support a new restriction.
- **(E)** If found true, the court may continue or change the restriction. If found not true, the
  restriction ends.
- **(F)** Good-faith reports are protected. "Unsubstantiated" does not mean "false." Good-faith
  protective action is not "failure to facilitate."
- **(G)** A knowingly false allegation (material falsity, knowledge, and purpose) has three
  consequences:
  - a change in circumstances and a best-interest factor;
  - compensatory time;
  - fees.
- **(H)** Excludes protection orders and Chapter 2151 cases themselves. Preserves 2151.421.

## PART FIVE — ENFORCEMENT

### R.C. 3109.051(K) (amended)
- **(K)(1)** On a contempt finding, compensatory time is **mandatory** (unless not in the child's
  best interest, with written reasons): same type and duration, used within one year. Costs and fees
  are already mandatory.
- **(K)(2)** A hearing within 28 days. Prima facie case: the order, the time that should have
  happened, and evidence it didn't. The burden of producing a lawful excuse shifts; the burden of
  persuasion does not.
- **(K)(3)** The single lawful-excuse list:
  - emergency, illness, weather, transportation, or circumstances outside the person's control;
  - an agreed change;
  - compliance with another order;
  - good-faith protective action that is promptly reported.
- **(K)(4)** Child refusal:
  - reasonable efforts are defined as conduct;
  - no physical force and no compelled statement;
  - refusal alone is not an excuse;
  - the court may still consider a mature child's wishes.
- **(K)(5)** A third contempt finding is a change in circumstances, and fees may not be waived.
- **(K)(6)** The court may refer conduct to the prosecutor. A parent may still go directly to
  police.

## PART SIX — CRIMINAL INTERFERENCE

### R.C. 2919.23 (amended): interference with custody
- **(E)(1)** It is an offense to **knowingly** keep a child from a parent during court-ordered time:
  - 3 or more periods in 90 days;
  - 14 or more consecutive days; or
  - any period after a contempt finding within the past 2 years.
- **(E)(2)** Lawful excuses (the 3109.051(K)(3) list), and a reasonable reading of an unclear order.
  The state disproves lawful excuse beyond a reasonable doubt.
- **(E)(3)** Plain withholding is prosecuted under (E), not (A)(1), unless the child is concealed or
  taken out of Ohio.
- **(E)(4)** **Not "a civil matter."** Civil remedies do not bar investigation or prosecution
  (*Toledo v. Parra*).
- **(D)(5)** Penalty: first-degree misdemeanor. Fifth-degree felony after a prior conviction, or if
  the child is concealed or taken out of Ohio.
- **(G)** Court referrals. Within 60 days (one self-executing extension of 30), the prosecutor files
  a charge or sends a written declination. No reasons are required. There is no review, and no bar to
  a later prosecution.

## PART SEVEN — UNCODIFIED

- **Section 3, Existing orders:**
  - the presumption applies to allocations decided after the effective date, including in pending
    cases;
  - existing orders stand;
  - the status motion is available;
  - enactment is otherwise not a change in circumstances;
  - for restrictions already in place, the 36-month clock ends at the later of 36 months or 12
    months after enactment;
  - the enforcement and criminal provisions apply to conduct after the effective date.
- **Section 4, Child support:** calculation is unchanged. A status order is not by itself grounds to
  modify support.
- **Section 5, Data:** the Supreme Court is requested to report:
  - equal-time outcomes;
  - status motions;
  - referrals and declinations.
- **Section 6, S.B. 174:** a statement of intent to conform if S.B. 174 is enacted.
