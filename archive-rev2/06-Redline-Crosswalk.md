## 6. Allegations

| Current law | Change | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| No limit on how long a custody-court restriction can rest on an unresolved allegation | § 3109.046: 90-day hearing on request; 36-month cap; a 30-month self-set hearing; one continuance of up to 90 days | Instruction (the 36 months); OH R.C. 2151.35(B)(1) (90-day disposition, a single capped extension, then dismissal) as the structural model |
| Agency dispositions (Ohio Adm.Code 5180:2-1-01) are often treated as findings | "Resolved" requires a court finding after a hearing; agency dispositions, charging decisions, and acquittals do not count | NEW; uses existing standards only |

## Ideas from H.B. 14 / H.B. 508 that were **not** adopted, and why

| Idea | Bill | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| Clear and convincing rebuttal; "detrimental" test | H508 / H14 as introduced | The most effective objection against both bills |
| Majority of time to the parent "more likely to honor and facilitate" after rebuttal | H508 3109.0425 / H14 3109.0424–.0425 | A second contest layered on the first; invites friendly-parent weaponization |
| Policy statements (3109.044) | H508 / H14 | R.C. 3109.401 already exists |
| ~60 new sections, renumbering, repeal of 3109.051 | H14 | The size itself was an objection |
| Statewide standard parenting-time guidelines by rule | H508 3109.0466 | Replaced by a one-sentence floor; avoids a separation-of-powers target |
