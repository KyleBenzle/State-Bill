# DOCUMENT 9 — COURT-WORKLOAD AND FISCAL MEMO (REVISION 5)

**The test:** does a provision remove a decision someone now asks a judge to make?

## 1. Removed from judges and magistrates

| Today | Under the bill | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| A contempt hearing for each missed weekend | The FSEA decides; unopposed determinations are entered without a hearing | 3109.047 |
| Make-up time argued case by case | Mandatory, same type and duration | 3109.047(D); 3109.051(K) |
| Implementation disputes litigated | Parenting coordinator first, where one exists | 3109.047(B) |
| Years of history reconstructed | One enforcement record per order | 3109.047(G) |
| An open-ended best-interest contest | A presumption with defined rebuttal grounds | 3109.044(D) |
| Uncertain weight of an older child's wishes | A reasoned preference at 12+ is followed unless a stated exception applies | 3109.044(F) |
| An unmarried father must sue for status | Status by operation of law | 3109.042 |
| Fee, deposit, and indigency disputes over required services | A clear rule: agreed, requested, or free | 3109.053 |
| Disputes over who decided a schedule change | Only a court or the parents' agreement | 3109.045 |
| Indefinite allegation-based restrictions | One clock, one resolution | 3109.046 |

Revision 5 removes one item from Revision 4's list ("sanction amounts by schedule"), because the
payments are deleted. Make-up time does the same work without a money dispute.

## 2. What moves to the FSEA

Parenting complaints (intake, order retrieval, notice and response, determinations, make-up time, the
record, referral, contempt filing at thresholds) and support accounting (requests, records,
redaction, shortfall determinations).

## 3. What remains judicial

Contempt and jail; custody and status changes; safety findings; protection orders; unclear orders;
objections to FSEA determinations; first allocations; restoration motions; component designations
beyond child care and deviations (on request); credits (entered by the court); criminal guilt.

## 4. New work

**Courts:** objections to FSEA determinations; ministerial entry of unopposed ones; a one-time wave of
restoration motions and first schedule motions; 90-day and 30-month allegation hearings (pulled
forward, not added); component designation requests (mostly at order entry or modification).

**FSEAs:** Part 2. New, and not IV-D reimbursable (45 CFR 304.20(b)).

**Courts and counties:** free court-required services, now including GALs.

## 5. Fiscal

| Item | Direction | Note |
|---|---|---|
| FSEA parenting complaints | Cost (state and county) | Volume unknown; Michigan's Friend of the Court is the nearest model |
| FSEA accountings | Cost | Capped at one per order per year; where-not-what keeps review simple |
| Support-system change (component field) | One-time cost | Covered by the one-year delayed start |
| **Free GALs, coordinators, evaluators, classes** | **Largest cost** | Applies only where a parent did not agree or request. Courts may stop requiring paid services. **Statewide GAL fee volume is the key unknown** for LSC |
| Court contempt hearings | Savings | Routine denials go to the FSEA |
| Payments (Rev. 4) | — | Deleted; no collection or set-off administration |
| Offsets | Partial | 42 U.S.C. 669b (small Ohio share) where legally available; existing local resources |

**Net:** a modest court-workload reduction after a two-to-three-year transition. A real state and
county cost, led by the service-cost rule and FSEA staffing. No parent-paid FSEA fee; no IV-D misuse;
no new statewide office.
