# PART TWO — THE STARTING POINT

## Sec. 3109.044 — enacted. Shared parenting and substantially equal time.

**(A) Presumption.** When a court first allocates parental rights and responsibilities for a child,
including by a temporary order or an order under section 3109.12 of the Revised Code, there is a
presumption that shared parenting with substantially equal parenting time is in the child's best
interest. "Substantially equal" means each parent has at least forty-five per cent of overnights, or
the equivalent, over a year.

**(B) Order if not rebutted.** If the presumption is not rebutted, the court shall issue a shared
parenting decree under which both parents are residential parents and legal custodians, major
decisions are made jointly, and parenting time is substantially equal. No party needs to file a
plan; if none is filed, the court shall issue one.

**(C) When the presumption does not apply.** The presumption does not apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the parents agree. The court shall approve their agreement unless it finds the
agreement is not in the child's best interest;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** As to a parent against whom a protection order was issued after a full hearing under
section 2151.34, 2903.214, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code to protect the other parent or the child,
while the order is in effect;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** As to a parent described in the second paragraph of division (C) of section 3109.04 of
the Revised Code.

An ex parte protection order, or a consent agreement without findings, governs according to its terms
while it is in effect. It does not by itself rebut the presumption.

**(D) Rebuttal.** The presumption is rebutted only if the court finds, by a preponderance of the
evidence, one or more of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Substantially equal time would endanger the child's physical or emotional welfare,
including because of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or a parent's substance abuse or impairment;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A parent is unable or unwilling to exercise substantially equal time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Distance or the child's school attendance makes substantially equal time
impracticable;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** A parent has repeatedly denied the other parent court-ordered parenting time without
lawful excuse;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** A child has stated a reasoned preference for a different schedule that the court
follows under division (F) of this section.

None of the following is a ground: conflict between the parents; one parent's objection; a lack of
cooperation caused by the parent relying on it; or an allegation the court has not found true. When
the presumption applies, the factors in division (F)(1) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code are
considered only as they bear on these grounds.

**(E) If rebutted.** If the presumption is rebutted, the court shall:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** State in writing the facts establishing the ground;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Unless the parents agree, order a schedule that maximizes each parent's time with the
child, consistent with the child's welfare and division (F) of this section;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Not give a parent fewer than ninety overnights a year, or the equivalent, unless the
parents agree, that parent asks for less, distance makes it impracticable, the court follows a
child's preference under division (F) of this section, or the court finds by a preponderance of the
evidence that more time would endanger the child's physical or emotional welfare. In the last case,
the court shall order the least restrictive arrangement that protects the child and state its
reasons in writing.

Ninety overnights is a floor, never a standard or target. That a parent receives fewer than ninety
overnights is not, by itself, evidence of an offense under section 2919.23 of the Revised Code.

**(F) Child's preference.** A child age twelve or older may state a reasoned preference about
parenting time in an interview under division (B) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code. The court
shall follow the preference unless it finds in writing that following it would endanger the child or
be impracticable, or that the preference is not the child's own reasoned view. No child shall be
required to state a preference. The court, not the child, decides the allocation.

**(G) Decision-making.** The court may find the presumption rebutted as to parenting time but not as
to joint decision-making, or the reverse. Each parent makes day-to-day and emergency decisions while
the child is with that parent. If the parents cannot agree on a major decision, either may ask the
court to decide it, and the existing arrangement continues until the court does.

**(H) Safety.** A court may restrict a parent's rights or parenting time to protect the child or a
parent from physical harm, abuse, or domestic violence on a finding by a preponderance of the
evidence. A criminal charge or conviction is not required.

# PART THREE — ONLY A COURT CHANGES A COURT ORDER

## Sec. 3109.045 — enacted. Court orders control.

**(A)** An order allocating parental rights and responsibilities or providing for parenting time
remains in effect until a court changes it, except that the parents may agree to a particular
deviation. The agreement does not change the order.

**(B)** No one else may reduce, suspend, supervise, condition, or substantively change parenting time
or parental rights and responsibilities. That includes a guardian ad litem, parenting coordinator,
mediator, evaluator, counselor, therapist, law enforcement officer, and any employee of a public
children services agency or family support enforcement agency. Those persons may investigate,
recommend, mediate, assist, or carry out an order. A court shall not delegate that authority to
anyone who is not a judge or magistrate. An order provision that purports to do so is unenforceable.

**(C)** A guardian ad litem's recommendation is evidence only. A party may call the guardian ad litem
as a witness and cross-examine the guardian ad litem about the report and the basis for any
recommendation.

**(D)** A parenting coordinator may decide disputes about carrying out an order within the authority
given by the appointing order, but may not make a substantive change. On a parent's objection, the
court shall review the decision independently.

**(E)** A public children services agency safety plan restricts court-ordered parenting time only if
the parent agrees in writing or a court orders it.

**(F)** Nothing in sections 3109.044 to 3109.047 of the Revised Code limits a protection order, taking
a child into custody under section 2151.31 of the Revised Code, an emergency or ex parte court order,
or action reasonably necessary to prevent imminent physical harm to a person.

# PART FOUR — ALLEGATIONS MUST BE RESOLVED

## Sec. 3109.046 — enacted. Allegations; 36-month limit subject to one 45-day extension; false allegations.

**(A) Definitions.** In this section:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** "Restriction" means a court order, or part of one, that suspends, requires supervision
of, or materially reduces a parent's parenting time or parental rights and responsibilities because
of an allegation of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or other misconduct by that parent.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** An allegation is "resolved" when a court, after a hearing at which the restricted
parent could present evidence, finds whether it is true. The finding may come from that court, an
adjudication under Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code, a full protection-order hearing, or a criminal
conviction. An agency determination, a charging decision, a dismissal, or an acquittal does not by
itself resolve an allegation.

**(B) No restriction without an order.** An allegation, report, or filing does not by itself change a
parenting order. Only a court order based on evidence can impose a restriction.

**(C) Hearing on request.** On the restricted parent's request, the court shall hold an evidentiary
hearing on the allegation within ninety days unless good cause is shown. A pending investigation or
criminal case, or the absence of a charge, does not by itself prevent the court from deciding whether
the allegation is true.

**(D) The 36-month limit.** A restriction based on an unresolved allegation ends thirty-six months
after it first took effect, subject to one extension:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the allegation is unresolved at thirty months, the court shall set it for hearing
on its own motion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Before the thirty-six months end, the court may extend the period once, by not more
than forty-five days, on written findings that additional time is genuinely necessary.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Delay requested or caused by the restricted parent is not counted.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** All allegations about conduct before the restriction took effect share its period,
however later described. Only later conduct can support a new restriction with a new period.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** When the restriction ends, the order that applied before it resumes. If there was
none, the court shall issue a parenting time order under section 3109.044 of the Revised Code within
twenty-eight days, without relying on the unresolved allegation.

**(E) After resolution.** If the court finds the allegation true, it may continue, change, or end the
restriction in the child's best interest. If it finds the allegation not true, the restriction ends.

**(F) Good-faith reports.** An allegation or report made in good faith shall not be held against the
person who made it. That an allegation was unsubstantiated, uncharged, dismissed, acquitted,
mistaken, or not proven does not by itself establish that it was knowingly false. A parent's
good-faith action to protect the child or the parent from abuse or domestic violence shall not be
counted against that parent under division (F)(1)(f) of section 3109.04 or division (D)(10) of
section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(G) Knowingly false allegations.** If the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that a parent
made an allegation that was materially false, that the parent knew was false, and that the parent
made in order to interfere with the other parent's relationship with the child, the court shall
order compensatory parenting time for the time lost and the parent's payment of the other parent's
reasonable attorney's fees and costs. The finding is a change in circumstances for purposes of
division (E)(1)(a) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code.

**(H) Scope.** This section does not govern a protection order proceeding or a case under Chapter
2151. of the Revised Code. It does govern a restriction in a case under this chapter that relies on
either of them. It does not limit section 2151.421 of the Revised Code.

## Sec. 3109.047 — enacted. Parenting order enforcement.

**(A) Role.** A parent may ask the family support enforcement agency for help enforcing a parenting
time order. Use of the agency is optional, and a parent may instead bring a contempt action directly.
The agency does not decide custody, status, safety, or contempt, and it may not change an order.

**(B) Complaint.** A parent may file a complaint with the agency of the county where the child lives,
on a form prescribed by the department of job and family services, within sixty days after
court-ordered parenting time was missed. There is no fee. A dispute within the authority of an
appointed parenting coordinator goes first to the coordinator, and the agency proceeds if it is not
resolved within fourteen days. That step does not apply to a denial of fourteen or more consecutive
days or to concealment or removal of the child.

**(C) Inquiry.** Within fourteen days, the agency shall obtain the order from the court and send the
complaint to the other parent, who has fourteen days to respond. The agency may contact both parents and
offer a conference.

**(D) Determination.** Within forty-five days after the complaint, the agency shall issue a written
determination. If the order clearly provides the time, the time was not provided, and the other
parent has shown no lawful excuse under division (K)(3) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code, the
agency shall find that parenting time was denied. A parent who does not respond has shown no excuse.
For each denial, the agency shall specify compensatory parenting time of the same type and duration,
to be taken within one year at times the wronged parent chooses on seven days' notice. Otherwise, the
agency shall find no denial.

**(E) Matters for the court.** If the order is unclear as to the time in question, or a parent asserts
in good faith that the parenting time would endanger the child, the agency shall make no
determination. It shall file the complaint and its record with the court, which shall treat the
filing as a contempt action under division (K) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(F) Court entry and review.** The agency shall mail the determination to both parents, send it by
any electronic means a parent has provided, and file it with the court. Either
parent may object by filing with the court within fourteen days. The court shall hear the objection
within twenty-eight days and decide the matter anew. If no one objects, the court shall enter the
determination as its order unless it is contrary to law. An entered determination is enforceable like
any other order.

**(G) Record.** The agency shall keep one enforcement record for each parenting time order, showing the
time ordered and the time received as reported in each complaint, and every response, determination,
objection, outcome, and compensatory period. The agency shall file the record in any court proceeding
about parenting time or parental rights and responsibilities under that order.

**(H) Referral and contempt.** The agency shall refer the matter to the prosecutor under division (F)
of section 2919.23 of the Revised Code, and may bring a contempt action under section 2705.031 of the
Revised Code on the wronged parent's behalf, if any of the following applies:
- within twelve months, three denials by the same parent under the same order have been entered or
  found in contempt;
- a denial lasted fourteen or more consecutive days;
- the child was concealed or removed from this state.

**(I) No money remedy.** This section creates no fine, fee, penalty, or damages, and does not limit
any remedy otherwise available by law.

# PART SIX — CHILD SUPPORT FOR THE CHILD

## Sec. 3119.024 — enacted. Parental support and accountable child support.

**(A) Components.** Every child support order shall state the amount of parental support and the
amount of accountable child support. Both are child support for all purposes of the Revised Code and
federal law, including Title IV-D of the Social Security Act, income withholding, arrearages, and
Chapter 3115. of the Revised Code. Neither is spousal support.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** "Parental support" is support of the child paid to the parent or caretaker with whom
the child lives, for the child's general support through the household, including housing,
utilities, food, transportation, household costs, and caregiving. It is not subject to accounting.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** "Accountable child support" is support to be spent on or saved for the child for the
expenditures listed in division (C) of this section. It is subject to accounting under division (D)
of this section.

**(B) Designation.** Accountable child support consists of:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** The child care cost included in the calculation;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Any amount added by deviation under section 3119.22 of the Revised Code for an
expenditure listed in division (C) of this section;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Any further amount the parents agree to, or the court designates on a party's request,
not exceeding the amount the court finds the child reasonably requires for the expenditures listed in
division (C) of this section.

The remainder of the obligation is parental support. Cash medical support and health care coverage
are ordered as they are now and are not part of either component.

**(C) Qualifying expenditures.** Accountable child support is accounted for by spending on the child
for clothing; school tuition, fees, supplies, and tutoring; child care; extracurricular
activities, lessons, camps, and equipment; child-specific medical costs not otherwise allocated; and
deposits into an account held only for the child.

**(D) Accounting.**
- **Request.** Once in any twelve months, the obligor may request, through the family support
  enforcement agency, an accounting for the preceding twelve months.
- **Response.** Within sixty days, the obligee shall give the agency records showing where the
  accountable child support was spent: the payee, date, and amount of each payment the obligee
  identifies as made for the child.
- **What counts as a record.** A bank, credit card, or payment-app statement is sufficient. So is an
  invoice or payment confirmation. For a cash payment, the obligee's signed statement of the payee,
  date, and amount is sufficient. The obligee is not required to itemize purchases or produce
  receipts.
- **Delivery.** The agency shall provide the records to the obligor without any address or
  information the court has ordered withheld.
- **Exemption.** This division does not apply to support assigned to the state.

**(E) Shortfall.** If the obligee does not account for all accountable child support received in the
period, the agency shall determine the unaccounted amount. Division (F) of section 3109.047 of the
Revised Code applies to that determination. On entry, the unaccounted amount is credited, dollar for
dollar, against the obligor's accountable child support due over the next twelve months. The credit
is a deviation under section 3119.22 of the Revised Code, and the entered determination is the
finding that section requires.

**(F) No self-help.** The obligor shall pay the full order until a credit is entered. An accounting
dispute does not suspend, reduce, or excuse support. Division (E) of this section does not modify any
support already due.

**(G) Interstate orders.** Divisions (D) and (E) of this section apply only while a tribunal of this
state has continuing, exclusive jurisdiction to modify the order under section 3115.205 of the
Revised Code.
