# DOCUMENT 2 — DESIGN MEMO (REVISION 4)

**4 October 2026.** Revision 4 builds on Revision 3, which is archived in `archive-rev3/`.

**Labels used below:**
- **[LAW]** verified current law;
- **[MODEL]** another state;
- **[REC]** this memo's recommendation.

**The model:**
- Courts decide genuine legal, custody, and safety disputes.
- The Family Support Enforcement Agency (FSEA) administers routine enforcement.
- GALs investigate and recommend.
- Prosecutors decide charges.
- Parents parent.

## Part 2. The Family Support Enforcement Agency

**Structure [REC].** Each county's existing CSEA (R.C. 3125.10) is *also* designated its FSEA by one
sentence added to 3125.10. Considered and rejected:
- **Renaming the CSEA throughout the Code.** It would mean hundreds of conforming amendments and
  would risk federal IV-D designation (R.C. 3125.11).
- **A new state office.** It means new personnel and a new bureaucracy.

The same staff and buildings administer both functions:
- **IV-D functions** are unchanged.
- **FSEA functions** (parenting enforcement under § 3109.047 and support accounting under § 3119.024)
  are declared non-IV-D and separately accounted for. 45 CFR 304.20(b) limits federal matching funds
  to IV-D plan activities [LAW].

**Powers.** The Revision 3 analysis carries forward (Document 7, Part 6):
- **Contempt is judicial** (*Hale*; *Zakany*), so the FSEA cannot find contempt.
- **Determinations bind only when the court enters them,** which it does absent an objection within
  14 days. This is Ohio's support-review model (R.C. 3119.63–.65) and administrative paternity model
  (3111.49) [LAW].

**New in Revision 4:**
- **Parenting coordinator first.** A dispute within a parenting coordinator's authority goes to the
  coordinator first, for 14 days. This does not apply to long denials, concealment, or other remedies.
- **Standardized escalating payments: $100, $250, $500** per entered denial in 24 months, paid to the
  wronged parent.
  - *Tested models:* Arizona (A.R.S. 25-414, up to $100 per violation), Minnesota (518.175 subd. 6, up
    to $500), and Illinois (750 ILCS 5/607.5, fines per incident). All are court-imposed [MODEL].
  - *Here:* the FSEA calculates the amount; **the court enters it**, so the court is the body
    imposing it.
  - It is a civil judgment. It is **not support**, and it may not be set off against support
    (3109.05(D)).
- **Tracking.** The record shows time ordered against time received, as reported in each complaint.
  Monitoring every family was rejected as impractical.
- **No parent-paid FSEA fee.**

## Part 3. Parental support and accountable child support

**Federal constraint [LAW].** 42 U.S.C. 659(i)(2) defines "child support" as support "of a child …
**or a child and the parent with whom the child is living**." So:
- **Parental support** is defined as support of the child paid to the parent for the child's
  household. It is therefore federal child support.
- **Both components are "child support for all purposes"** (3119.01 amendment). That keeps
  withholding, IV-D enforcement, arrears, and UIFSA intact.
- **Parental support is never framed as support of the parent.** That would drift toward the
  "alimony" definition (659(i)(3)).

**The tension, stated plainly.** The instruction's rule is "if it is called child support, it is
accountable." Federal compatibility requires the *umbrella* to stay "child support."
- **[REC]** Inside the umbrella, the order names two components. The accountable one is called
  "accountable child support"; the other is "parental support."
- A court that wants an unaccounted transfer designates it parental support.

**Amount.** The guideline total is unchanged (R.C. 3119.021 schedule; 45 CFR 302.56 compliance
preserved).
- **The split:** accountable child support is the greater of **30 percent** of the total or the child
  care amount. The remainder is parental support. The court may vary the split on written findings.
- **Why 30:** USDA (2015) puts the child-specific categories (clothing 6%, child care and education
  16%, miscellaneous 7%) at **29 percent** of child-rearing spending [LAW / federal data]. No prior
  Ohio "30 percent rule" was found, and none is claimed.
- **Medical support** stays separate (R.C. 3119.30 et seq.), which avoids duplication.

**Qualifying expenditures:**
- clothing;
- school costs;
- child care;
- activities, camps, and equipment;
- child-specific medical costs not otherwise covered;
- **deposits into an account for the child**, so frugality is never penalized.

There is no grocery or utility accounting; those are covered by parental support.

**Trigger [REC].** Once a year, on request through the FSEA, without a court motion and without a
good-cause showing.
- *Contrast:* Louisiana (R.S. 9:312) and Missouri require a motion and good cause [MODEL]. The annual
  cap does the anti-harassment work instead.
- Sixty days to respond.
- **Records show where, not what [REC].** The obligee shows the payee, date, and amount of each
  payment made for the child.
  - A bank, credit card, or payment-app statement is enough, as is an invoice or payment confirmation.
    Cash payments are listed in a signed statement.
  - **No itemized purchases and no receipts.** This removes the main burden and privacy objection and
    the opponents' line ("they want mothers to keep receipts"). The answer is in the text: "The
    obligee is not required to itemize purchases or produce receipts."
  - *Trade-off:* a statement shows a payment to a general retailer, not what was bought. The obligee's
    identification of the payment as for the child is accepted. A knowingly false identification can
    be addressed through the court on objection.
- The FSEA forwards the records with protected addresses redacted.
- Support assigned to the state is exempt, as in Louisiana 9:312(D).

**Remedy [REC].** A **prospective credit** of the unaccounted amount against the next 12 months of
accountable child support. It is entered by the court absent objection (the FSEA model).
- No reduction of arrears, which the Bradley Amendment (42 U.S.C. 666(a)(9)) and R.C. 3119.83 forbid.
- No self-help.
- Payment continues in full until a credit is entered.
- *Alternatives:* reclassification, or redirecting future accountable support into a child account.
  These are Document 10, Decision 3.

## Part 4. Narrowing the best-interest standard

The narrowest change that stops open-ended balancing from swallowing the presumption:
- **Five enumerated grounds** (§ 3109.044(D)):
  1. endangerment, including abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or impairment;
  2. a parent's inability or unwillingness to exercise equal time;
  3. distance or schooling;
  4. repeated denial of the other parent's court-ordered time;
  5. the reasoned wishes of a child 14 or older, interviewed under 3109.04(B).
- **The (F)(1) factors still matter, but only as they bear on these grounds.**
- **"Best interest" still governs where the presumption does not apply:** agreements, full-hearing
  protection orders, 3109.04(C) offenders, and later modifications.
- **Policy finding in 3109.401:** fit parents ordinarily determine their children's interests, and
  courts, GALs, and third parties are not presumed better judges. This is the *Troxel* principle,
  stated as legislative policy.
- **Kept from Revision 3:** written findings, the maximize-time duty, and the 90-night floor.

**Why include ground (5)?** Without it, a 16-year-old's considered refusal could not count. It is the
most-asked question in any presumption debate. The age is a sponsor choice (Document 10, Decision 2).

## Part 5. No pay to parent

R.C. 3109.053 currently **requires** courts to charge parents for ordered classes and counseling
[LAW].
- **Revision 4** forbids conditioning rights, time, or status on paying for any court-required
  service: classes, counseling, evaluations, parenting coordination, supervised time, and exchanges.
- **The court may require a service only if it is free to the parent.** This is not a funding
  mandate. A court without a free service simply cannot impose that condition.
- **Preserved:** filing fees, costs, fee awards, restitution, and remedial orders against a parent the
  court found committed abuse, domestic violence, or interference.
- **Conforming amendments:** 3109.055 (conciliation) and 3109.04(C) (evaluations).
- **GAL fees are left alone** (Decision 4). They are a large cost item, and a GAL serves the court
  rather than being a service a parent is ordered to use.

## Part 6. False allegations

Settled: **clear and convincing** evidence of three elements, which are material falsity, knowledge,
and purpose to interfere.

Unsubstantiated, uncharged, dismissed, acquitted, mistaken, or unproven does not itself establish
falsity. There is no new crime (R.C. 2921.14 exists).

## Part 7. Parenting violations and support: no linkage

Researched; **none is drafted** [LAW]:
- R.C. 3109.05(D) forbids "escrowing, impoundment, or withholding of any child support payment …
  because of a denial of … parenting time."
- Federal IV-D enforcement and funding attach to support only.
- Any FSEA support-enforcement remedy triggered by parenting violations would run against both.

The bill's parenting remedies (make-up time, escalating payments, contempt, referral, and change of
circumstances) stand alone. The support obligation is untouched.

## Part 8. Protection orders

- **A full-hearing protection order** (2151.34, 2903.214, 3113.31) switches the presumption off as to
  the respondent while it is in effect.
- **An ex parte order, or a consent agreement without findings,** controls by its own terms while in
  effect. It does not itself rebut the presumption, because it is an allegation-stage order. The
  full hearing follows within 7 or 10 court days (R.C. 3113.31) [LAW].
- **Emergency authority is preserved** (3109.045(E)).
- **Consistency with § 3109.046:** a full-hearing finding is a "resolution" there too.

The treatment of consent agreements is a sponsor choice (Decision 5).
