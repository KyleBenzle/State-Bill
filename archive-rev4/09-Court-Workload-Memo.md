# DOCUMENT 9 — COURT-WORKLOAD AND FISCAL MEMO (REVISION 4)

**The test:** does a provision remove a decision someone now asks a judge to make?

## 1. Removed from judges and magistrates

| Today | Under the bill | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| A contempt hearing for each missed weekend | The FSEA decides; unopposed determinations are entered without a hearing | 3109.047 |
| Make-up time argued case by case | Mandatory, same type and duration; the FSEA calculates it | 3109.047(D); 3109.051(K) |
| Sanction amounts argued case by case | A standard $100 / $250 / $500 schedule | 3109.047(D)(2) |
| Implementation disputes litigated | A parenting coordinator first, where one exists | 3109.047(B) |
| Years of history reconstructed | One enforcement record per order | 3109.047(G) |
| An open-ended best-interest contest | A presumption with five defined rebuttal grounds | 3109.044(D) |
| An affirmative finding needed for an agreed plan | Approved unless harmful | 3109.044(C)(1) |
| An unmarried father must sue for status | Status by operation of law | 3109.042 |
| Fee and indigency disputes over court-ordered classes | No charge; the service is free or not required | 3109.053 |
| "How was support spent?" motions (in states that allow them) | An annual accounting through the FSEA, with no motion | 3119.024 |
| Ex parte protection orders used to decide custody | They control only while in effect; the full hearing decides | 3109.044(C) |
| Indefinite allegation-based restrictions, and repeat motions | One clock, one resolution | 3109.046 |

## 2. What moves to the FSEA

Parenting complaints:
- intake;
- order retrieval;
- notice and response;
- determinations;
- make-up time;
- payment calculation;
- the record;
- referral to the prosecutor;
- contempt filing for the parent.

Support accounting:
- requests;
- collection and redaction of documentation;
- shortfall determinations.

## 3. What remains judicial

- Contempt and jail.
- Custody and status changes.
- Safety and abuse findings.
- Protection orders.
- Unclear orders.
- Objections to FSEA determinations (decided anew within 28 days).
- First allocations.
- Equal-status motions.
- The first restored-parent schedule motion.
- Criminal guilt.

## 4. New work

**For courts:**
- objections to FSEA parenting and accounting determinations;
- ministerial entry of unopposed determinations;
- status motions (a one-time wave);
- first restored-parent schedule motions;
- 90-day and 30-month allegation hearings, which pull existing cases forward rather than adding
  cases;
- GAL cross-examination (marginal).

**For FSEAs:** all of Part 2. This work is new, and it is not federally reimbursable under Title IV-D
(45 CFR 304.20(b)).

**For counties and courts:** the no-pay rule. It either creates demand for free services or ends
paid-service conditions. It does not mandate spending.

## 5. Net assessment

**Courts: modest net reduction after a two-to-three-year transition.** The transition bump is
existing-order cases, from status motions plus first schedule motions. The steady-state savings come
from:
- routine enforcement moving to the FSEA;
- defined rebuttal grounds producing more settlements;
- accounting and service-fee disputes handled without motions;
- allegation disputes ending by rule.

**Fiscal: the cost moves to the state and counties.**
- **FSEA staffing** for parenting complaints and support accountings. Accountings could be numerous;
  the annual cap limits volume.
- **Free court-required services** where courts choose to keep requiring them.
- **Offsets:**
  - 42 U.S.C. 669b Access and Visitation grants (about $10 million a year *nationally*, a small Ohio
    share), "to the extent legally available";
  - existing local resources;
  - no new court-funding structure.
- **There is no parent-paid FSEA fee.** The escalating payments go to the wronged parent, not to the
  agency.
- **Expect an LSC fiscal note** showing a material state or county cost. That is the bill's principal
  political exposure. The one-year delayed start (Section 3(E)) allows appropriation and planning.
