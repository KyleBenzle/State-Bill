# PART TWO — THE STARTING POINT

## Sec. 3109.044 — enacted. Shared parenting and substantially equal time.

**(A) Presumption.** When a court first allocates parental rights and responsibilities for a child,
including by a temporary order, there is a presumption that shared parenting with substantially
equal parenting time is in the child's best interest. "Substantially equal" means each parent has at
least forty-five per cent of overnights, or the equivalent, over a year.

**(B) Order if not rebutted.** If the presumption is not rebutted, the court shall issue a shared
parenting decree under which both parents are residential parents and legal custodians, major
decisions are made jointly, and parenting time is substantially equal. No party needs to file a
plan. If none is filed, the court shall issue one.

**(C) When the presumption does not apply.** The presumption does not apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the parents agree. The court shall approve their agreement unless it finds the
agreement is not in the child's best interest;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** As to a parent against whom a protection order was issued after a full hearing under
section 2151.34, 2903.214, or 3113.31 of the Revised Code to protect the other parent or the child,
while the order is in effect;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** As to a parent described in the second paragraph of division (C) of section 3109.04 of
the Revised Code.

An ex parte protection order, or a consent agreement without findings, governs according to its terms
while it is in effect. It does not by itself rebut the presumption.

**(D) Rebuttal.** The presumption is rebutted only if the court finds, by a preponderance of the
evidence, one or more of the following:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** Substantially equal time would endanger the child's physical or emotional welfare,
including because of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or a parent's substance abuse or impairment;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** A parent is unable or unwilling to exercise substantially equal time;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Distance or the child's school attendance makes substantially equal time
impracticable;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** A parent has repeatedly denied the other parent court-ordered parenting time without
lawful excuse;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** A child age fourteen or older, interviewed under division (B) of section 3109.04 of the
Revised Code, has reasoned wishes that substantially equal time would not serve.

None of the following is a ground: conflict between the parents; one parent's objection; a lack of
cooperation caused by the parent relying on it; or an allegation the court has not found true. When
the presumption applies, the factors in division (F)(1) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code are
considered only as they bear on these grounds.

**(E) If rebutted.** If the presumption is rebutted, the court shall:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** State in writing the facts establishing the ground;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Unless the parents agree, order a schedule that maximizes each parent's time with the
child, consistent with the child's welfare;

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Not give a parent fewer than ninety overnights a year, or the equivalent, unless the
parents agree, that parent asks for less, distance makes it impracticable, or the court finds by a
preponderance of the evidence that more time would endanger the child's physical or emotional
welfare. In that case, the court shall order the least restrictive arrangement that protects the
child and state its reasons in writing.

**Ninety overnights is a floor, never a standard or target.** That a parent receives fewer than
ninety overnights is not, by itself, evidence of an offense under section 2919.23 of the Revised
Code.

**(F) Decision-making.** The court may find the presumption rebutted as to parenting time but not as
to joint decision-making, or the reverse. Each parent makes day-to-day and emergency decisions while
the child is with that parent. If the parents cannot agree on a major decision, either may ask the
court to decide it, and the existing arrangement continues until the court does.

**(G) Safety.** A court may restrict a parent's rights or parenting time to protect the child or a
parent from physical harm, abuse, or domestic violence on a finding by a preponderance of the
evidence. A criminal charge or conviction is not required.

# PART THREE — ONLY A COURT CHANGES A COURT ORDER

## Sec. 3109.045 — enacted. Court orders control.

**(A)** An order allocating parental rights and responsibilities or providing for parenting time stays
in effect as written until a court changes it. No one else may change it, suspend it, or add
conditions to it. That includes a parent, guardian ad litem, parenting coordinator, mediator,
evaluator, counselor, therapist, law enforcement officer, and any employee of a public children
services agency or family support enforcement agency. Their recommendations, reports, allegations,
safety plans, and instructions do not change the order. Parents may agree to change a particular
exchange or period. That agreement does not change the order.

**(B)** A guardian ad litem may investigate, interview, observe, obtain information, report, testify,
and make recommendations as provided by law and the Rules of Superintendence. A recommendation is
evidence. It has no effect unless the court adopts it in an order. A party may call the guardian ad
litem as a witness and cross-examine the guardian ad litem about the report and the basis for any
recommendation. A court shall not give a guardian ad litem, or anyone else who is not a judge or
magistrate, authority to change, suspend, supervise, or condition parenting time or the allocation
of parental rights and responsibilities. A provision of an order that purports to give that
authority is unenforceable.

**(C)** A parenting coordinator may help parents carry out an order and decide disputes about how it
is carried out, within the authority given by the appointing order and the Rules of Superintendence.
A parenting coordinator may not reduce or suspend parenting time, require supervision, or change the
allocation of parental rights and responsibilities. On a parent's objection, the court shall review
the decision independently.

**(D)** A public children services agency safety plan restricts a parent's court-ordered parenting
time only if that parent agrees in writing or a court orders it. An agency that believes a child is
in danger may seek an emergency order under Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code.

**(E)** Nothing in sections 3109.044 to 3109.047 of the Revised Code limits a protection order, taking
a child into custody under section 2151.31 of the Revised Code, an emergency or ex parte court order,
or action reasonably necessary to prevent imminent physical harm to a person.

# PART FOUR — ALLEGATIONS MUST BE RESOLVED

## Sec. 3109.046 — enacted. Allegations; 36-month limit subject to one 45-day extension; false allegations.

**(A) Definitions.** In this section:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** "Restriction" means a court order, or part of one, that suspends, requires supervision
of, or materially reduces a parent's parenting time or parental rights and responsibilities because
of an allegation of abuse, neglect, domestic violence, or other misconduct by that parent.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** An allegation is "resolved" when a court, after a hearing at which the restricted
parent could present evidence, finds whether it is true. The finding may come from the court that
issued the restriction, an adjudication under Chapter 2151. of the Revised Code, a full hearing on a
protection order, or a criminal conviction. None of the following, by itself, resolves an
allegation: an agency's determination, a decision whether to charge, a dismissal, or an acquittal.

**(B) No restriction without an order.** An allegation, report, or filing does not by itself change a
parenting order. Only a court order based on evidence can impose a restriction.

**(C) Hearing on request.** On the restricted parent's request, the court shall hold an evidentiary
hearing on the allegation within ninety days unless good cause is shown. A pending investigation or
criminal case, or the absence of a charge, does not by itself prevent the court from deciding whether
the allegation is true.

**(D) The 36-month limit.** A restriction based on an unresolved allegation ends thirty-six months
after it first took effect, subject to one extension. The following rules apply:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the allegation is unresolved at thirty months, the court shall set it for hearing
on its own motion.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** Before the thirty-six months end, the court may extend the period once, by not more
than forty-five days, on written findings that additional time is genuinely necessary.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Delay requested or caused by the restricted parent is not counted.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(4)** Every allegation about conduct that occurred before the restriction took effect shares
the restriction's period, however the allegation is later described. Only conduct after that date
can support a new restriction with a new period.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(5)** When the restriction ends, the order that applied before it resumes. If there was
none, the court shall issue a parenting time order under section 3109.044 of the Revised Code within
twenty-eight days, without relying on the unresolved allegation.

**(E) After resolution.** If the court finds the allegation true, it may continue, change, or end the
restriction in the child's best interest. If it finds the allegation not true, the restriction ends.

**(F) Good-faith reports.** An allegation or report made in good faith shall not be held against the
person who made it. That an allegation was unsubstantiated, uncharged, dismissed, acquitted,
mistaken, or not proven does not by itself establish that it was knowingly false. A parent's
good-faith action to protect the child or the parent from abuse or domestic violence shall not be
counted against that parent under division (F)(1)(f) of section 3109.04 or division (D)(10) of
section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(G) Knowingly false allegations.** If the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that a parent
made an allegation that was materially false, that the parent knew was false, and that the parent
made in order to interfere with the other parent's relationship with the child, the court shall
order:
- compensatory parenting time for the time lost;
- the parent's payment of the other parent's reasonable attorney's fees and costs.

The finding is a change in circumstances for purposes of division (E)(1)(a) of section 3109.04 of the
Revised Code.

**(H) Scope.** This section does not govern a protection order proceeding or a case under Chapter
2151. of the Revised Code. It does govern a restriction in a case under this chapter that relies on
either of them. It does not limit section 2151.421 of the Revised Code.

## Sec. 3109.047 — enacted. Parenting order enforcement.

**(A) Role.** The family support enforcement agency administers parenting time orders. It does not
decide custody, status, safety, or contempt, and it may not change an order.

**(B) Complaint.** A parent may file a complaint with the agency of the county where the child lives,
on a form prescribed by the department of job and family services, within sixty days after
court-ordered parenting time was missed. There is no fee.

If a parenting coordinator has been appointed and the dispute is within the coordinator's authority,
the parent shall first submit it to the coordinator. The agency proceeds if the dispute is not
resolved within fourteen days. The coordinator step does not apply to a denial of fourteen or more
consecutive days, to a concealment or removal of the child, or to any other remedy.

**(C) Inquiry.** Within fourteen days, the agency shall obtain the order from the court and send the
complaint to the other parent, who has fourteen days to respond. The agency may contact both parents,
gather information, and offer a conference.

**(D) Determination.** Within forty-five days after the complaint, the agency shall issue a written
determination:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1)** If the order clearly provides the time, the time was not provided, and the other parent
has shown no lawful excuse under division (K)(3) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code, the agency
shall find that parenting time was denied. A parent who does not respond has shown no excuse.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2)** For each denial, the agency shall specify:
- compensatory parenting time of the same type and duration, to be taken within one year at times the
  wronged parent chooses on seven days' notice;
- payment by the denying parent to the other parent of one hundred dollars for the first denial
  entered within any twenty-four months, two hundred fifty dollars for the second, and five hundred
  dollars for each later denial.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(3)** Otherwise, the agency shall find no denial.

**(E) Matters for the court.** If the order is unclear as to the time in question, or a parent asserts
in good faith that the parenting time would endanger the child, the agency shall make no
determination. It shall file the complaint and its record with the court, which shall treat the
filing as a contempt action under division (K) of section 3109.051 of the Revised Code.

**(F) Court entry and review.**
- The agency shall send the determination to both parents by ordinary mail, and by any electronic
  means a parent has provided, and shall file it with the court.
- Either parent may object by filing with the court within fourteen days. The court shall hear the
  objection within twenty-eight days and decide the matter anew.
- If no one objects, the court shall enter the determination as its order unless it is contrary to
  law.
- An entered determination is enforceable like any other order. A payment under division (D)(2) of
  this section is a civil judgment. It is not support and shall not be collected from or set off
  against support.

**(G) Record.** The agency shall keep one enforcement record for each parenting time order. The record
shows the time ordered and the time actually received as reported in each complaint, and every
response, determination, objection, outcome, payment, and compensatory period. The agency shall file
the record in any court proceeding about parenting time or the allocation of parental rights and
responsibilities under that order.

**(H) Referral.** The agency shall refer the matter to the prosecutor under division (F) of section
2919.23 of the Revised Code, and may begin a contempt action for the wronged parent under section
2705.031 of the Revised Code, if any of the following applies:
- within twelve months, three denials by the same parent under the same order have been entered or
  found in contempt;
- a denial lasted fourteen or more consecutive days;
- the child was concealed or removed from this state.

# PART SIX — PARENTAL SUPPORT AND ACCOUNTABLE CHILD SUPPORT

## Sec. 3119.01 — amended. Definitions.

**(C)** … {+ (__) "Parental support" and "accountable child support" have the meanings in section
3119.024 of the Revised Code. Both are child support for all purposes of the Revised Code and federal

## Sec. 3119.024 — enacted. Parental support and accountable child support.

**(A) Two components.** Every child support order shall state the obligation in two components:

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(1) "Parental support"** is support of the child paid to the parent or caretaker with whom the
child lives, for the child's household. It covers housing, utilities, food, transportation,
household costs, and caregiving. The recipient is not required to account for it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;**(2) "Accountable child support"** is support that must be spent on the child, or saved for the
child, and accounted for under division (C) of this section.

**(B) Amount.** The total obligation is calculated under this chapter as it is now. Accountable child
support is the greater of thirty per cent of the total obligation or the child care cost included in
the calculation. The remainder is parental support. The court may designate a different division on
written findings. Cash medical support and health care coverage are ordered and accounted for as they
are now, and are not part of either component.

**(C) Qualifying expenditures.** Accountable child support is accounted for by expenditures for the
child for the following:
- clothing;
- school tuition, fees, supplies, and tutoring;
- child care;
- extracurricular activities, lessons, camps, and equipment;
- child-specific medical costs not covered under division (B) of this section;
- deposits into an account held only for the child.

**(D) Accounting.**
- **Request.** Once in any twelve months, the obligor may request, through the family support
  enforcement agency, an accounting for the preceding twelve months.
- **Response.** Within sixty days, the obligee shall give the agency records showing where the
  accountable child support was spent: the payee, date, and amount of each payment the obligee
  identifies as made for the child.
- **What counts as a record.** A bank, credit card, or payment-app statement is sufficient. So is an
  invoice or payment confirmation. For a cash payment, the obligee's signed statement of the payee,
  date, and amount is sufficient. **The obligee is not required to itemize purchases or produce
  receipts.**
- **Delivery.** The agency shall provide the records to the obligor without any address or
  information the court has ordered withheld.
- **Exemption.** This division does not apply to support assigned to the state.

**(E) Shortfall.** If the obligee does not account for all accountable child support received in the
period, the agency shall determine the unaccounted amount. Division (F) of section 3109.047 of the
Revised Code applies to that determination. On entry, the unaccounted amount is credited against
the obligor's accountable child support due over the next twelve months.

**(F) No self-help.** The obligor shall pay the full order until a credit is entered. An accounting
dispute does not suspend, reduce, or excuse support. Division (E) of this section does not modify any
support already due.

  Revised Code ends on the later of thirty-six months after the restriction took effect or twelve
  months after the effective date, subject to one extension.
- (D) Sections 2919.23, 3109.047, and 3109.051(K) of the Revised Code, as enacted or amended by this
  act, apply to conduct on or after the effective date, whatever the date of the order.
- (E) Sections 3109.047 and 3119.024 of the Revised Code take effect one year after the effective date
  of this act. Section 3119.024 applies to a child support order issued or modified on or after that
  date. Either party may ask the agency to restate an existing order in the two components without
  changing its total.

**Section 4. Total support unchanged.** This act does not change how the total child support
obligation is calculated. An order under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code is
not by itself grounds to modify child support.

**Section 5. Forms, funding, and data.**
- (A) The General Assembly requests that the Supreme Court adopt statewide forms for a shared parenting
  plan and a motion under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code.
- (B) The department of job and family services shall adopt the forms for sections 3109.047 and
  3119.024 of the Revised Code. It shall fund those sections and the services required by division (B)
  of section 3109.053 of the Revised Code from:
  - state appropriations;
  - existing local resources;
  - to the extent legally available, federal access and visitation grants under 42 U.S.C. 669b.

  No Title IV-D funds shall be used for a purpose federal law does not permit.
- (C) The General Assembly requests that the Supreme Court publish each year:
  - the number of initial allocations, and how many ordered substantially equal time;
  - the number of motions under division (E)(1)(c) of section 3109.04 of the Revised Code, and how many
    were granted;
  - agency complaints, determinations, objections, and referrals;
  - prosecutorial declinations;
  - accounting requests and entered credits.

**Section 6. S.B. 174.** If Sub. S.B. 174 of the 136th General Assembly is enacted, the General Assembly
intends this act to be conformed to it.
