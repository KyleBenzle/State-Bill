# DOCUMENT 4 — PLAIN-ENGLISH SUMMARY (REVISION 4)

*This describes a proposed bill. It is not law.*

### 1. Every legal parent is an equal parent.
Once paternity is legally established, **both parents are equal legal custodians**, married or not.
You don't have to sue. Equal status isn't a schedule: either parent can ask a court for one, with a
hearing within 28 days on a temporary order.

### 2. The starting point is 50/50.
At the first custody decision, the court starts from **shared parenting and substantially equal time**
(at least 45 percent each). The law says fit parents, not judges or GALs, ordinarily decide what's
best for their children. If you agree on a plan, the judge approves it unless it's bad for your child.

**The 50/50 start can be overcome only by showing one of five things:**
1. it would endanger the child;
2. a parent can't or won't take equal time;
3. distance or school makes it impractical;
4. a parent has repeatedly denied the other's court-ordered time;
5. a child 14 or older has reasoned wishes against it.

Conflict between the parents, one parent's objection, or an unproven accusation isn't enough.

**If 50/50 is overcome:**
- the judge explains why in writing and gives each parent as much time as possible;
- **no parent gets fewer than 90 nights a year** unless the parents agree, the parent asks for less,
  distance prevents it, or more time would endanger the child.

**Ninety nights is a floor, never a goal.**

**Protection orders:** a protection order issued *after a full hearing* takes the 50/50 start away
from the person it's against, while it lasts. A short-term emergency (ex parte) order controls while
it's in effect, but on its own it doesn't decide custody.

### 3. Old custody order? You can be an equal parent again.
File a motion. You don't have to prove that circumstances changed. It's granted unless you have a
proven history of violence or abuse, or you're an immediate threat.

**Your schedule, support, and the child's school stay the same** until a judge changes them. You
can't just take the child. **Your first request for more time starts from 50/50.**

### 4. Only a judge changes a court order.
Not a GAL, a parenting coordinator, a counselor, a caseworker, or a police officer. A GAL's
recommendation is only advice to the judge, and you can question the GAL in court.

**You can't be made to pay to parent.** If a court requires a class, counseling, an evaluation, a
parenting coordinator, or supervised visits or exchanges, it must be free to you. Otherwise the court
can't require it.

### 5. A county agency enforces your parenting time.
Your county child support agency becomes the **Family Support Enforcement Agency (FSEA)**.
1. File a free complaint within 60 days of missed time. If you have a parenting coordinator, routine
   issues go there first, for 14 days.
2. The FSEA gets the order, asks the other parent to respond, and decides within 45 days.
3. If time was wrongly denied, you get **make-up time** of the same kind and length. The other parent
   also pays you **$100**, then **$250**, then **$500** for repeat denials within two years.
4. Either parent can object to a judge within 14 days. If nobody objects, the judge signs it.

**Valid excuses:**
- emergencies, illness, weather, or car trouble;
- changes you both agreed to;
- following a protection order;
- a good-faith protective step that is promptly reported.

Safety claims and unclear orders go straight to a judge.

The FSEA keeps **one record** of time ordered against time received. **Three denials in a year**, 14
days or more of withholding, or a hidden or out-of-state child means an automatic referral to the
prosecutor. **Three denials in two years** are grounds to change custody.

**Support and parenting time stay separate.** Ohio law already forbids holding back support because
parenting time was denied, and the bill keeps that rule.

### 6. Child support gets two parts.
The total stays the same as today's calculation. The order now splits it:
- **Parental support** goes to the household: rent, utilities, food, transportation. No records are
  needed.
- **Accountable child support** is at least **30 percent** (or the child-care cost, if higher). It
  must go to the child: clothing, school, child care, activities, camps, and equipment. Money put into
  savings for the child counts too.

Once a year, the paying parent can ask, through the FSEA, for an accounting. The other parent has 60
days to show **where** the money went: the place, date, and amount of each payment. A bank or credit
card statement is enough, and cash payments can be listed and signed. **No receipts and no itemized
lists of what was bought.** Any amount not accounted for is **credited against future**
accountable support.

**You may never stop or reduce payments yourself.** Nothing changes support already owed.

### 7. Accusations must be decided.
- An accusation alone changes nothing; only a judge can restrict you.
- You can demand a hearing within 90 days.
- A restriction based on an undecided accusation ends after **36 months, subject to one 45-day
  extension**. The judge must schedule a hearing at 30 months.
- Delays you cause don't count, and rewording an old accusation doesn't restart the clock.

**Honest reports are protected.** "Unsubstantiated," "not charged," "dismissed," or "acquitted"
doesn't mean "false." A **knowingly false** accusation, proven by **clear and convincing evidence**,
brings make-up time, legal fees, and grounds to change custody.

### 8. Serious withholding is a crime, not "just civil."
- Keeping a child during the other parent's court-ordered time, without a valid excuse, is
  interference with custody under existing Ohio law.
- An honest reading of an unclear order isn't a crime.
- After a referral, the prosecutor has 60 days (90 with one extension) to charge or send a written
  notice of declining. No reasons are required. A referral is not proof of guilt.
