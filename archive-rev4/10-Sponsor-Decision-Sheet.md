# DOCUMENT 10 — SPONSOR DECISION SHEET (REVISION 4)

Only genuinely open choices are listed. In each case, Option A is what the draft does.

---

**1. The accountable share of child support**
- **A.** The greater of 30 percent of the total or the child care amount.
- **B.** A lower share (about 15–20 percent) covering clothing, activities, and education only, with
  child care accounted for separately.
- **Recommendation: A.** It rests on federal data (USDA, 29 percent child-specific) and is one
  objective rule. B accounts for less money and puts less accounting burden on recipients.

**2. The child's-wishes rebuttal ground**
- **A.** The reasoned wishes of a child 14 or older, given in a judicial interview.
- **B.** Age 12, or no child-wishes ground at all.
- **Recommendation: A.** Without some ground, an older teenager's considered view cannot count. With
  a lower age, the ground invites pressure on children.

**3. The remedy for unaccounted accountable child support**
- **A.** A prospective credit against the next 12 months of accountable child support.
- **B.** Redirect future accountable child support into an account held for the child.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is simplest and is self-executing through FSEA entry. B keeps the money
  for the child but adds account administration.

**4. GAL fees under the no-pay-to-parent rule**
- **A.** Excluded. GAL fees stay taxable as costs under existing law.
- **B.** Included. GAL services become free to parents, or GALs are not appointed.
- **Recommendation: A.** B is the largest fiscal item in the bill and would reduce GAL use sharply. A
  middle option: bar only conditioning a *hearing* on payment of a GAL deposit.

**5. Consent protection-order agreements**
- **A.** Like ex parte orders: they govern by their terms but do not switch off the presumption.
- **B.** Like full-hearing orders: they switch off the presumption as to the respondent.
- **Recommendation: A.** A consent agreement contains no finding. B would discourage respondents from
  consenting and push more cases to contested hearings.

**6. The escalating payment for entered denials**
- **A.** $100, then $250, then $500 per denial within 24 months, payable to the wronged parent.
- **B.** No payment: make-up time, contempt, and referral only.
- **Recommendation: A.** A standardized consequence deters denial without a court hearing. B removes
  the strongest administrative incentive.

**7. Agency first, or the parent's choice**
- **A.** A parent may use the FSEA *or* file contempt directly.
- **B.** The FSEA route is required before contempt, except in emergencies.
- **Recommendation: A at enactment.** Revisit after two years of data. Requiring exhaustion before
  FSEAs are staffed would delay relief.

---

*Settled and no longer open:*
- clear-and-convincing standard for knowingly false allegations;
- 90-night floor;
- 50/50 presumption on the first restored-parent schedule motion;
- no pre-order no-removal rule;
- the FSEA as the enforcement body;
- no parent-paid FSEA fee;
- R.C. 2919.23(A)(1) and *Parra* intact;
- no support-enforcement linkage (prohibited by R.C. 3109.05(D)).
