## 4. The enumerated rebuttal grounds (R4)

**Attack.** "Limiting rebuttal to five grounds prevents a court from protecting a child's best
interest in an unforeseen case."

**Answer:**
- **Ground (1) is broad.** It covers any endangerment of physical or emotional welfare, so genuine
  harm always qualifies.
- **The presumption does not apply** to agreements, full-hearing protection orders, or 3109.04(C)
  offenders.
- **The grounds narrow, not eliminate, judgment.** They track factors Ohio courts already weigh
  (3109.04(F)(1)). They bar only reliance on *non-harm* preferences once the presumption applies.

**On *Troxel*.** The 3109.401 finding strengthens the bill's posture: it adopts the
fit-parent presumption as policy.

**Risk: low-moderate.** The bill is more rigid than the enacted states, which rebut on best interest.

## 6. FSEA

Same analysis as Revision 3, which used the identical structure:
- determinations bind only on court entry, after a 14-day objection window, with review decided anew
  (the R.C. 3119.65 / 3111.49 model);
- no contempt power (*Hale*; *Zakany*);
- safety claims go to court.

**R4, designation by addition.** Designating the CSEA as FSEA by adding a sentence leaves IV-D
designation (3125.11) intact. The non-IV-D accounting requirement in 3125.10 addresses 45 CFR
304.20(b).

**R4, parenting coordinator first.** This is a 14-day step, and only within the coordinator's
existing authority. It is exempt for serious cases, and the coordinator's decisions stay
court-reviewable (*Gregory*).

**Risk: low-moderate.**

## 7. Escalating payments (R4)

**Attack.** "A $100/$250/$500 escalating payment for disobeying a court order is punishment.
Punishment for disobedience is contempt, which requires a court finding, notice, and the protections
of 2705.031–.05."

**Answer:**
- **The court imposes it.** The FSEA only calculates. The court enters the payment as a civil
  judgment after notice, a 14-day objection window, and an opportunity for a hearing decided anew.
- **The legislature may set civil consequences for violating court orders.** Ohio already makes
  costs and fees mandatory on contempt (3109.051(K)). Arizona (A.R.S. 25-414), Minnesota (518.175
  subd. 6), and Illinois (607.5) prescribe court-imposed civil penalties of comparable size [MODEL].
- **It is payable to the wronged parent**, which supports characterizing it as compensatory.

**The residual weakness.** The escalation looks punitive.
- *Fallback if challenged:* the payments are severable, and make-up time survives.
- *Option:* recharacterize them as liquidated compensation, with a flat amount.

**Risk: moderate.**

## 8. Parental support and accountable child support (R4)

**Federal definitions.** Both components are "child support" under 42 U.S.C. 659(i)(2), which covers
support of "a child and the parent with whom the child is living." The 3119.01 amendment declares
them child support for all federal and state purposes. So withholding, IV-D enforcement, and arrears
are unaffected.

**Guidelines (45 CFR 302.56).** The total is computed exactly as now. Labeling components does not
change the guideline amount or its presumptive correctness.

**Bradley Amendment (42 U.S.C. 666(a)(9); R.C. 3119.83).** The accounting credit applies only to
*future* accountable installments, entered by court order. Arrears are never reduced.

**Distribution.** Collections are still distributed under 42 U.S.C. 657. The accounting duty is a
post-receipt duty of the obligee. It does not alter distribution. Support assigned to the state is
exempt.

**UIFSA.** The money obligation is a "support order" (R.C. 3115.102) and is enforceable anywhere.
Whether another state will enforce the *accounting term* on registration is **unverified**. If it does
not, the term is enforceable in Ohio only, which is acceptable.

**Due process (obligee).**
- The annual cap limits burden.
- Accountings go through the FSEA, with protected addresses redacted.
- Only payee, date, and amount are required. Card or bank statements suffice, and there is no
  itemizing and no receipts.
- Savings for the child qualify.
- The credit requires court entry after an opportunity to object.

**Policy attack.** "This burdens low-income custodial parents, mostly mothers, and invites
harassment."
- *Answer:* the annual limit; no court motion; records show where money went, not what was bought,
  so a card statement is enough; assigned support is exempt.
- *Remaining:* the 30 percent share and the documentation burden are political exposures (Document 10,
  Decision 1).

**Interstate and federal reporting.** OCSE reporting treats the order as one child support amount.
Systems may need a component field. That is an implementation item, which is why the start is delayed
one year.

**Risk: moderate.** This is novel in Ohio, but each federal constraint is addressed in the text.

## 10. False allegations (R4)

Clear and convincing evidence of all three elements, plus express protection for good-faith,
unsubstantiated, dismissed, uncharged, and acquitted reports, sharply reduces chilling.

**Risk: low** (was low-moderate).

## 12. No pay to parent (R4)

**Attack (separation of powers).** "Courts control their own services."

**Answer.** The bill regulates *conditions on parental rights*, which is substantive. It does not
direct court spending. A court may still order any service it can provide free.

**Attack (unfunded mandate).** None in form, since a court may simply stop requiring a paid service.
In practice, demand for free services rises.

**Preserved:** costs, fees, restitution, and remedial orders after a court finding of misconduct.

**Risk: low-moderate**, mostly fiscal and political.

## 13. Protection orders (R4)

**Victim safety:**
- A full-hearing order defeats the presumption while in effect.
- An ex parte order governs by its own terms (custody and contact) immediately, and the full hearing
  follows within 7 or 10 court days (R.C. 3113.31).
- Emergency authority is preserved (3109.045(E)).

**Respondent fairness.** An allegation-stage order does not permanently defeat equality.

**Consent agreements.** Treated like ex parte orders (Document 10, Decision 5).

**Risk: low.**

## 14. Funding (R4)

- Title IV-D funds are not used for FSEA parenting or accounting functions (45 CFR 304.20(b); CRS
  IF13043).
- 669b funds are used only "to the extent legally available."
- No court-funding change.
- No parent-paid FSEA fee.

**Risk: low** legally. The cost is political (Document 9).
